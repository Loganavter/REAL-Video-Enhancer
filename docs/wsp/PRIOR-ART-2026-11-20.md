# Prior art for this lane: what exists, and what it changes

Searched 2026-11-20. Web search was unavailable (HTTP 403), so this is the GitHub search API plus the arXiv API,
i.e. repositories and papers by name — not a code search (that needs auth) and not a systematic review. Treat the
absences as "not found", not as "does not exist".

## The one that maps onto the open question

**PAMS — Quantized Super-Resolution via Parameterized Max Scale** (ECCV 2020, arXiv:2011.04212).
A *trainable truncated parameter* per layer explores the upper bound of the quantisation range, plus a structured
knowledge-transfer loss to fine-tune. Their stated problem is that SR models without batch norm have a large
dynamic quantisation range; their result: 8-bit PAMS-EDSR *improves* PSNR over the FP32 baseline on Set5
(32.095 -> 32.124 dB) at 2.42x compression.

This is our grid clamp `s[c] = max(s_in[c], s_t/K)` with `K` **learned per layer instead of swept**. It is the
answer to the question this lane has been circling for two days: the optimum K is interior and model-dependent
(measured: 2, 3 or 4 depending on the model, and the weight term has an interior minimum), no weight statistic
predicts it (five tried, all overlapping), and a linear-in-errors argmin scores 4/22. PAMS says: stop predicting
it, make it a parameter and let training place it.

## The next step past per-channel grids

**DFSQ — Distribution-Flexible Subset Quantization for Post-Quantizing SR Networks** (arXiv:2305.05888, code at
`zysxmu/DFSQ`). Motivated by exactly what we measured - "activation distributions exhibit significant variance
across samples and channels" - it does **channel-wise normalisation of activations** and then quantises onto a
*subset of a universal set* of multi-word additive log-scale points, with a k-means-based selection.

Where our lane is: per-tensor vs per-channel vs clamp, all on a **uniform** int8 grid. DFSQ's point is that the
grid need not be uniform; a log-scale subset spends codes where the distribution is. That is orthogonal to our
axis and is the obvious next lever if the picture ever needs more than the clamp delivers.

## Calibration frames are a known lever - and we are not clip-limited

**CFQP — Towards Clip-Free Quantized SR: How to Tame Representative Images** (BMVC 2023, arXiv:2308.11365).
The representative dataset that sets the quantisation range is the overlooked step: they augment it with FP32
model outputs to eliminate clipped activations, and report up to 54% runtime reduction (mobile SR models carry
clipped activations specifically to survive PTQ) with better visual quality than clipped INT8 models.

Two consequences for us: our "spread frames, 24 of them" decision is the right family of answer; and our measured
clipping (0.00000-0.00001% of codes beyond +-127 in both grid modes) says we are not in the regime this paper
fixes. So it validates a choice, it does not open a lever.

## The "train for int8" route, with a cost

**AIO_MAI — Efficient INT8 SR via Deployment-Aware Quantization and Teacher-Guided Training** (CVPRW 2026,
arXiv:2604.20291, `NguyenPhamPhuongNam/AIO_MAI`). LR-space extract-refine-upsample with a MobileOne-style
re-parameterisable backbone, three-stage training, **QAT applied to the fused deploy graph** ("deploy-before-QAT")
plus teacher distillation, exported to INT8 TFLite. Their INT8 costs **0.15 dB PSNR** against their FP32 student
(30.28 -> 30.13).

That is the price of int8 when it is *trained for*, on a model built for it - a useful reference point next to our
own: our int8 body costs 0.0114% of pixels on three frames and 0.00353% on a 494-frame pair against the engine,
with no training at all. Different statistics, but the orders are comparable and it says our PTQ path is not
obviously behind a QAT path.

Also note the structural echo: MobileOne's re-parameterisable blocks are SPAN's `Conv3XC` idea, and their
"deploy-before-QAT" is our "calibrate and quantise the deployed arithmetic" - both confirm that folding to the
deploy graph *before* choosing quantisation parameters is the standard move, which is what our body already does.

## SPAN specifically: nobody has done it

The official repo (`hongyuanyu/SPAN`, 286 stars, active) has an `inference/` directory containing per-architecture
inference scripts (basicvsr, dfdnet, esrgan, ridnet, stylegan2, swinir) - **no ONNX export, no TensorRT, no
quantisation, no deployment path for SPAN itself**. So our scope finding stands unchallenged: 65.5% of the frame is
3x3 convolution, 32.2% is attention/concat/shuffle, and there is no prior implementation to copy or to refute.

## Deployment prior art that does not change our answer

**`AmusementClub/vs-mlrt`** - VapourSynth runtimes over TensorRT, ONNX Runtime, ncnn, OpenVINO, MIGraphX, CoreML,
with a Python wrapper and per-model helpers. It is the mature version of "run SR models in a video pipeline", and
its TensorRT backend can build int8 engines with calibration. But this lane already measured TensorRT's int8 for
*this* model and lost (`int8 implicit/FP8` closed by measurement, per the lane's own record), which is why the
Triton body exists. vs-mlrt is the right prior art for *deployment breadth*, not for the lever we are pulling.

## Concrete inventory: what exists for OUR model family

Searching by name for `SRVGGNetCompact` (the family our int8 body runs) returns **five repositories**, and none of
them is an int8 implementation for a desktop GPU:

| repo | what it is |
|---|---|
| `xocialize/realesrgan-mlx` (7*) | Apple MLX port of RRDBNet + SRVGGNetCompact, tile-based inference |
| `xocialize/realesrgan-mlx-swift`, `xocialize/mlx-realesrgan-swift` | the same for Swift/MLX |
| `zhonghonggg/Remote-Sensing-Super-Resolution` | ONNX Runtime DirectML/CPU, no quantisation |
| **`tomhda/vaiml-bf16-prelu-repro`** | **a minimal repro: VitisAI EP (VAIML BF16) silently miscompiles SRVGGNetCompact with trained PReLU weights on a Ryzen AI NPU** |

The last one matters as a warning rather than a tool: the *only* public artefact about a quantised
SRVGGNetCompact is a bug report whose trigger is **PReLU**. Our int8 hop applies the model's PReLU in the epilogue
before the requant, in fp16 - and our shares (0.0114% on three frames, 0.00353% on 494 against the engine) say it
matches, but the failure mode has a name now and deserves its own control: a body whose PReLU slope is forced to
1.0 should read *worse*, not the same, on a model whose slopes are trained.

### Two VSR quantisation papers that answer our per-layer question

- **QuantVSR** (AAAI 2026, arXiv:2508.04485): low-bit PTQ for real-world VSR, with a **spatio-temporal complexity
  aware** mechanism that uses the calibration dataset to measure per-layer spatial/temporal complexity and
  allocates layer-specific capacity, plus learnable bias alignment.
- **LSGQuant** (ICML 2026, arXiv:2602.03182): layer-sensitivity guided quantisation for one-step diffusion VSR, with
  a **dynamic range adaptive quantiser** for token activations and a **variance-oriented layer training strategy**
  that estimates layer sensitivity from calibration statistics.

Both are about diffusion VSR (much larger models) and both do what our predictor attempts failed to do: they get
per-layer sensitivity **from calibration statistics**, not from a weight-only summary and not from a single
global coefficient. That is exactly stage (b) of `rve-grid-k-learned` - a per-layer K with no training - and it is
the strongest signal in this search that the per-layer direction is where the field went.

### The mobile world quantises VSR routinely, and this is the scale of it

The **Mobile AI 2022 challenge report** (arXiv:2211.05256) on power-efficient VSR for NPUs reports solutions
"fully compatible" with a quantised NPU path at up to **500 fps** and 0.2 W per 30 fps on a Dimensity 9000. That is
the regime where int8 VSR is standard practice: small models, NPU, TFLite/QNN. Our lane is the desktop-GPU
counterpart of the same idea, with a different constraint (one frame at a time through a Triton kernel).

### Deployment prior art is fp16, not int8

`AmusementClub/vs-mlrt`'s TensorRT page documents `--fp16`, `--noTF32` and tactic sources - **no int8 path**, and
no calibration step, for any of its SR models. So the mature "SR in a video pipeline" stack runs fp16, and the int8
version of that idea is what this lane built itself.

## What I would take from this, in order

1. **Make the clamp a learned parameter (PAMS), not a swept one.** It removes the 0.77 s sweep, removes the guard,
   and removes the "which K for this model" question entirely - at the price of a training loop we do not have.
   The measurement that decides whether it is worth building: does a per-layer learned `s[c]` beat the best fixed K
   by more than the sweep's own spread? Our ladder says the spread between K=2 and the best K is up to 7x, so the
   headroom exists.
2. **Subset/non-uniform codebooks (DFSQ)** if the picture needs more: it is the next axis after the clamp, and our
   per-channel finding is exactly its motivation.
3. **Nothing for SPAN.** No prior art, and the scope arithmetic says the destination is behind the lane's start.
