# int8: the TRT plan's per-convolution rate (2026-11-17)

Moved out of `RESEARCH-LEDGER.md`. Verdict: `trt-int8-rate-79` (scope: TRT plan
`c2x_int8_fp16heads.plan` - NOT our kernel, which is `int8-triton-block`).

## E33 first pass: the int8 body runs at 79 TOPS, below the 91 the item quotes (measured 2026-11-17)

`scripts/probe_int8_tactics.py` on the int8 plan left from the 2026-11-16 lane
(`c2x_int8_fp16heads.plan`), same session, 20 executions x 3 rounds:

| | here | the record |
|---|---|---|
| frame | **67.67 ms** (67.70 / 67.65 / 67.69) | 64.3-70.4 ms, `engine-int8.txt` |
| layers | **84**, 1 myelin, **22 reformat**, **19 pointwise** | 84, 19-22 reformat, 19 PointWise |
| per-conv rate | median **79.2 TOPS**, min 76.2, max **119.7**, 18 conv layers | "91 TOPS" |

The reproduction of the shape and the time is exact, which is what makes the third row readable: the
convolutions are at **79 TOPS**, ~13% under the 91 the queue and E27's arithmetic both rest on. At 79
the 16-conv body floor is **30.9 ms**, not 26.9; at the 119.7 the fastest single layer reaches it would
be 20.4. So the gap E33 exists to close is a 1.5x on the GEMM, and the honest ceiling below it is
~1.5x, not the 1.6x the item wrote.

One instrument lesson, earned by the first run of this probe: the filter that computed per-layer rates
excluded only `Reformat`, so the int8 PReLU passes and fused arithmetic layers went into the same list
and one pointwise layer read **26,886 TOPS**. A pointwise layer does not have the convolution's FLOPs,
so it cannot be divided by them; the filter now requires a conv name and excludes `PWN`/`PointWise`/
`Cast`/`MoveAdd`/`Reformat`.

What this does **not** answer: whether a tiling level or tactic source raises 79 above 120 - the sweep
needs one build per configuration, and after the B3 finding each configuration needs at least three
builds before it can be compared. The item's 1-2 day estimate was written for one build per point.

Records: `docs/wsp/verified/int8-tactics.txt`.

## The depth-2 blocker, split by owner

`int8-composition-d2` asked for one control and named it: fp16(t-2) against fp16(t) on the same pairs.
It costs no int8, it was never run, and it decides which subsystem the next day goes to. It is run now,
three times, on the two clips the live lane used.

The probe prints it on the SAME pairs and at the SAME separation it reads the composition on, so only
the renderer changes. On the sample the product actually uses (`frames=16`, 8 test pairs):

| clip | d2 int8 | d2 fp16 | fp16 share of d2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chainsaw Man 20 s | 0.2177% | **0.1910%** | 88% |
| Violet Evergarden 20 s | 0.1219% | 0.0769% | 63% |

On Chainsaw the gate's own reference alone is above the 0.1% bar. A renderer with no quantisation error
would still be refused, so the fix there is the gate's certification, not the quantiser - the E25 gap,
now measured on the gate's own reference.

Then the sample size, which was the second surprise: each `frames` value samples different times, so
these are three populations, not one growing sample.

| clip | 8 pairs | 12 pairs | 16 pairs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chainsaw int8 d2 | 0.2177% | 0.1603% | 0.1748% |
| Chainsaw fp16 d2 | 0.1910% | 0.1389% | 0.1580% |
| Violet int8 d2 | 0.1219% | 0.0248% | 0.0265% |
| Violet fp16 d2 | 0.0769% | 0.0131% | 0.0045% |

Chainsaw's refusal survives every sample and is the gate's in every sample. Violet's refusal exists only
at 8 pairs - at 12 and 16 the same probe passes. So one of the two live refusals was the estimator, and
the product's decision rule is currently reading a number whose spread is the same order as its bar.

Two instrument defects came out on the way, both fixed rather than worked around:

- the probe held `ref` and `got` for every pair (~200 MB per 4K triple), so 16 triples sat at 11.2 GiB
  and a 32-triple run died with `CUDA out of memory`. The three statistics are now taken inside the
  per-pair loop; the sample size is bounded by time, not memory. Without this the 24- and 32-pair rows
  above could not exist.
- the depth-2 number and the control existed only as three-decimal text in the reason string. They are
  fields on `Int8ProbeResult` now, because a value that exists only rounded is not a record.

What is still not answered: the probe approximates the gate's served reference ("both block-mean steps
pass") rather than modelling the chain state and the pixel veto, and its pair order is mirrored - it
renders the earliest frame of the triple against later references, while the gate only reuses past
frames. Both are recorded in the evidence file rather than silently changed, because changing either
changes every d2 number above.

Records: `docs/wsp/verified/int8-d2-control.txt`, logs `docs/wsp/verified/logs/d2-control-f*.log`.

## The render control: the default exceeds the bar, and int8 was never the blocker

The d2 numbers above come from the probe, and the probe is not the render: it renders the earliest
frame of a decoded triple against later references, uses torch fp16 instead of the TensorRT plan, and
approximates the gate's served reference with block-mean steps. Any of those could read 0.19% where the
render reads 0.02%, so the number had to be taken on the product.

Two renders of the same clip through `backend/rve-backend.py`, lossless container, one with the shipped
auto gate (threshold 4.0, chain 3, 100/481 reused) and one with `--no_temporal_reuse`:

| | |
|---|---|
| worst frame (164) | **0.49372%** of pixels more than 8 levels off |
| frames above the 0.1% bar | **18 of 481** |
| peak on that frame | 14 levels |
| A/A - gated twice | 0.00000%, 481/481 decoded frames identical |

Only reused frames can differ (the render is deterministic and the A/A is exact), so all 18 are among
the 100 the gate reused. The gate's own certificate in the same run reads p90 0.73 / 0.69 / 0.66 at
depths 1-3 against limits 2 / 12 / 12 - and both statements are true, because a block-mean p90 on the
low-resolution input does not bound a per-pixel count on the high-resolution output. On the worst frame
the HR block-mean is 3.91 while 0.49% of pixels are off by more than 8.

So the fork resolved the first way: the shipped fp16 default exceeds the project's acceptance statistic
on this clip, and the fix is the gate's certification, not the quantiser. int8 waits.

## Correction: the bar was the gate's criterion all along

The verdict above first said the 0.1% bar "had never been applied to the gate". That is wrong. The bar
was derived *on a gate artifact* - the moved fist a viewer saw while VMAF min 93.65 and PSNR 52.7 dB
passed it - and the ledger states it as "0.009% for the shipping configuration and 0.287% for the
rejected tile configuration". The shipping numbers were measured against it: Chainsaw Man 8 s
**0.005%** (peak 14, 66.4% reuse), Violet 8 s **0.096%** (peak 18, 4% of margin), 502 anime frames
0.009-0.017%.

So the 20 s reading is not a first contact with a foreign criterion. It is a portability failure inside
one title:

| clip | reuse | share > 8 | peak |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chainsaw Man, 8 s at 5:00 | 66.4% | **0.005%** | 14 |
| Chainsaw Man, 20 s | 20.8% | **0.494%** | 14 |

The first version of this paragraph said "99x, and the peak did not move". Both halves were wrong. The
peak *did* move: 14 on the worst-by-share frame, but 75 on frame 432, so the clip's maximum peak went
from 14 to 75. And the 99x compares different populations - worst of ~15 frames in an 8 s window against
worst of all 481 - so a regression factor is not established. What stands is the product claim: 18 of
481 frames above the bar, A/A exact, and only reused frames can differ.

The ROC over those 100 reuses (below) also kills the simpler story. Less reuse, more defect, but not
because the gate is blind: its own delta ranks the damage at AUC 0.989.

## The gate's own variable on the worst frame

Fed the LR frames through the product's own `ReferenceFrameGate` with the parameters the run's log
reported; the counters reproduce the render exactly (100 reused, 2 scene cuts, 0 vetoes).

| frame | reuse | delta (levels) | chain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 161 | no | 249.78 (scene cut) | 0 |
| 162 | yes | 0.00 | 1 |
| 163 | yes | 0.83 | 2 |
| **164** | **yes** | **3.29** | **3** |

Frame 164 is served from frame 161's HR output - three frames stale, the deepest the cap allows - on a
decision that says the worst block moved by 3.29 levels. Markov on a block of 1024 pixels bounds the
share above 8 by `tau/8`, so 3.29 permits **41%**; certifying 0.1% needs a block mean of 0.008 levels,
500x below the threshold of 4.0 and 412x below what the frame read. The mean cannot be tightened into
safety.

The gate already runs `mean_abs`, the better of the two statistics the module measures (0.004% against
0.460% at threshold 8 on the moving fist), so the fix is not a switch between them. And the three
substitutions are independent: mean against count, LR against HR, luma against channel-max - the third
one alone is 8x on this artifact, which is why a chromatic change on a static frame is invisible to
this gate without any motion at all.

## The hooks: 722 ms a frame, and neither obvious cause

The guess was that the hooks hold full tensors. They do not - both reduce immediately to a 64-vector -
and removing the two device syncs per conv changes nothing (889 against 880 ms). The reduction itself
costs 2.20 ms on an idle GPU (1.2x its roofline); inside the forward the same call on the same
channels_last tensor measures 13.27 ms, charging the conv that produced it. 34 reductions at 2.2 ms
would be 75 ms against a measured 722 ms, so **the mechanism is not established**.

The prize is bounded: at their isolated cost the hooks would take 24 x (0.158 + 0.075) = 5.6 s instead
of 21.1 s, i.e. -15.5 s on a 45 s probe. No fix has been shown to recover it.

Records: `docs/wsp/verified/gate-decision-vs-acceptance.txt`, `hook-cost.txt`, `probe-cost.txt`.

## The ROC: the gate is not blind, and the one frame it cannot see is a highlight

Joined the gate's per-frame decisions with the per-frame HR share over the 100 frames it reused:

| feature | AUC | Spearman |
|---|---|---|
| LR share of pixels above 4 levels | **0.993** | +0.66 |
| the gate's own delta | **0.989** | +0.56 |
| LR max \|delta\| (the veto's variable) | 0.910 | +0.49 |
| chain depth | **0.507** | +0.09 |

So "the threshold cannot work" is wrong as stated: the threshold ranks the damage almost perfectly, and
the chain depth - which the frame-164 story leaned on - carries no information at all. Lowering delta
from 4.0 to 3.9 keeps 97% of the reuse and catches 15 of 18.

But the three frames with the highest delta are not the three worst frames. The worst by share, 164, has
a middling 3.294, and catching it needs 3.3 - which still leaves 9 bad frames and costs 10% of the
reuse.

The reason is that there are two classes:

| | 164 | 432 |
|---|---|---|
| share / peak | 0.4937% / 14 | 0.3053% / 75 |
| gate delta / chain | 3.294 / 3 | 3.935 / 2 |
| LR share above 8 | **0.000%** | 0.259% |
| LR max delta | **5.25** | 56.75 |
| shape | 40951 px in 63 full blocks, 8-16 levels | 25319 px over 379 blocks, up to 75 |
| reference luma at those pixels | **252 (100% above 240)** | 50 (p10 24, p90 107) |

Frame 164 is a saturated white region shifted 8-14 levels: no LR statistic sees it, the upscaler
manufactures the HR error from a smooth sub-threshold change, and every bad pixel sits at 252/255, where
8-14 levels are not 8-14 levels of visibility. Frame 432 is the moving-edge class at ordinary exposure -
the class the disabled pixel veto is aimed at: at 40 levels it keeps 82 of 100 reuses and catches 16 of
18.

So the next decision is not "threshold or statistic". It is: enable the veto (cheap, catches the
dominant class, costs 18% of reuse), and decide separately whether a highlight-only difference counts.

## Corrections that changed the conclusions

**The threshold table was inverted.** The columns "bad caught" and "bad left" were swapped in the ROC
write-up, which turned the finding upside down: at 3.0 the threshold blocks 15 of 18 bad frames for 16%
of the reuse and one good frame, instead of "9 left and 10% of the reuse". The corrected table:

| threshold | reuse kept | bad left | bad blocked | good lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4.0 (shipped) | 100% | 18 | 0 | 0 |
| 3.9 | 97% | 15 | 3 | 0 |
| 3.3 | 90% | 9 | 9 | 1 |
| 3.0 | 84% | 3 | 15 | 1 |
| 2.5 | 80% | 0 | 18 | 2 |

Forced and measured on the product, threshold 3.0 gives **6 frames above the bar instead of 18** at
18.3% reuse: 48.85 ms against 47.58, +2.7% of the wall, and all 12 dropped reuses were defective
frames. The worst frame survives because in the app's own luma its delta lands just under 3.0 where the
simulation reads 3.294.

**The probe's reference was not the fp16 model.** `_calibrate` loads int8-rounded weights and `ref` was
computed after it. The error is small (peak 4 levels, 0.00000% above 8) but it is not neutral: with the
reference taken from the original weights, Chainsaw's depth-2 moves by 0.02% and Violet's by **37-214%**
(0.122 -> 0.167 at 8 pairs, 0.026 -> 0.083 at 16). Violet's numbers were dominated by the quantiser and
the quantised reference had been hiding part of them; Chainsaw's are dominated by the reference and did
not move. The reference now comes from a second module holding the original weights, and the control
pass folded into the main loop (one pass instead of two).

**Frame 164's mechanism is chroma in a clipped highlight.** The difference is in the R channel alone -
gated 255 against ungated 244 at all 40951 bad pixels, G and B identical at 255. The stale reference
freezes a blown highlight one step whiter. That is why no LR statistic sees it: the change is in a
channel that is already at the ceiling, where an absolute 8-level threshold is blind by construction.

## The threshold fixes the render and not the verdict

The gate threshold at 3.0 removes 12 of 18 bad frames for +2.7% of the wall with no good frame lost.
It does not unblock int8, and the reason is structural rather than quantitative: the probe's depth-2
number on Chainsaw is **0.2177% at gate thresholds 3.0, 4.0 and 5.0**. At 3.0 the pair set shrinks from
two to one and the value is unchanged - the pair that carries the damage is already under every
threshold in the tuner's clamp.

So the pairs the gate certifies are exactly the pairs where the damage lives, which is the E25 gap
restated from the other side: no threshold on the gate's own variable can remove them. The levers that
remain are the statistic (the error sits in one saturated channel of a clipped highlight, where an
absolute 8-level threshold is blind) and a certificate taken on the rendered output.

Found on the way: the probe filtered pairs at 5.0 while the tuner clamps to (2.0, 4.0), so the probe
certified on a superset of what the gate can serve. Fixed as a parameter; it does not change the worst
here, but the population is the gate's now.

Records: `docs/wsp/verified/probe-threshold-invariance.txt`.

## The defect is in the projection, not the reduction

Recomputed the same LR statistics in channel-max over the same 100 reused frames:

| feature | AUC |
|---|---|
| share above 8 levels, any channel | **1.000** |
| share above 4 levels, luma (the gate's projection) | 0.993 |

Frame 164 is **rank 1 of 100** by the channel-max share above 8, against rank 27 by the luma share above
4. The class needs no second mechanism.

The arithmetic: frame 164 is a clipped highlight, ungated (244,255,255) against gated (255,255,255) - 11
levels in R, zero in G and B. The gate's luma taps are (1,2,1)/4, so R enters at 0.25 and the same
change reads 2.75 in the gate's variable against 11 in the acceptance variable: a factor of four, and it
is structural for any colour restored on a clipped highlight. The change is real in the LR input (mean
R delta 11.2 over the flagged area, max 15), not manufactured by the upscaler - that reading came from
the luma number.

Cost of a channel-max gate at the acceptance threshold: bad frames bottom at 0.0818% and good top at
0.0651%, so blocking above 0.08% takes all 18 bad and no good frame; reuse 17.0%, wall 49.50 ms against
47.58 = +4.0%, and the worst frame is gone - which the luma threshold at 3.0 could not do for +2.7%.

And the threshold really cannot substitute: the probe reads 0.2177% at gate thresholds 5.0, 4.0, 3.0 and
**2.0** - the whole clamp, measured rather than inferred.

Why the simulator and the app disagree at the boundary: the app converts YUV to RGB with
`cv2.COLOR_YUV2RGB_I420` (BT.601, full range), the simulator with ffmpeg (BT.709, tv range). The luma
taps are identical; the RGB underneath is not. The simulation reproduces the app's decisions at 4.0,
where the sign of delta-4 is not close, and diverges at the boundary (84 predicted against 88 measured
at 3.0). Every absolute threshold quoted from it must be re-derived on the app's own numbers.

Records: `docs/wsp/verified/gate-channel-max.txt`.

## The operating point: headroom, portability, and one refuted colour story

Three numbers decided the operating point, and one of them refuted my own explanation.

**The domains.** The separation is in LR shares; the bar is an HR share. Worst of the 82 survivors:
0.0651% LR, **0.0755% HR**. Best of the 18 bad: 0.0818% LR, 0.1013% HR. So the surviving population
sits at 0.0755%, leaving 0.0245 pp to the bar, and int8's measured increment on Chainsaw is 0.0267 pp -
**the gate fix alone lands about 0.002 pp over**, not under.

**Portability.** HR/LR over the 100 frames: median 2.04, p90 9.50, max 35.4. The mapping is not a
constant, so a fixed LR threshold is a fit to this clip's edge density. The tuner already measures the
real HR delta on its calibration frames; that is where the threshold belongs.

**The threshold.** 0.07% (1451 LR px) sits between the good max (1349) and the bad min (1696) with 14.4%
of margin, against 2.2% for 0.08% (1658 px, 37 px from the first missed defect). Same reuse, same wall,
six times the margin.

**The colour story was wrong.** I said the app converts BT.601 full-range while the simulator decodes
BT.709 tv, and that this explains the 84-vs-88 boundary divergence. Measured: `cv2.COLOR_YUV2RGB_I420`
against ffmpeg with explicit bt709/tv on the same frame differs by **max 3 levels, mean 0.93** - no
range error, no matrix error on this content, and bt601 against bt709 is identical here. So the
conversion is not the 12.68 dB class of bug, and the 3.294-vs-<3.0 discrepancy is **unexplained**.

**A third statistic, found on the way.** The gate uses `(1,2,1)/4` luma, the acceptance uses channel-max,
and the probe's pair filter uses `mean(R,G,B)` - a plain average that is neither. Three statistics, three
places.

## The channel-max gate, on the product

Implemented as `--temporal_reuse_statistic channel_max_share` (the share of LR pixels whose worst
channel moved by more than 8 levels - the bar's own quantity), forced past the auto tuner because the
tuner works in levels. Chainsaw Man 20 s, threshold 0.07%, chain 3:

| | luma gate | channel-max gate |
|---|---|---|
| reuse | 20.8% | **17.3%** |
| worst frame | 0.49372% | **0.07546%** |
| frames above 0.1% | 18 | **0** |
| frame 164 | 0.49372% | 0.00000% |
| wall | 47.58 ms | 49.37 ms projected (+3.8%) |

The worst frame is 0.07546% - the surviving maximum predicted from the 100-frame analysis (0.0755%), to
four digits.

The block-max form of the same statistic does **not** separate (AUC 0.992, bad min 3.4 against good max
4.6): the separating quantity is the *area* above the level, not the worst block. So the reduction had to
change as well, which neither of us had assumed.

With the probe's pair filter moved onto the same variable (it used `mean(R,G,B)`), Chainsaw reads
depth-2 0.0034% at 32 triples - 51x down from 0.1748% - and passes. Violet still fails on **depth 1**
(0.108%), where no reuse is involved: that is the quantiser on that material, not the gate. And at the
product's default 16 triples Chainsaw has no depth-2 pair at all, so its verdict there rests on depth 1.

And the new statistic is not more expensive: the gate's entry cost is 4.42 ms with channel-max against

## Verification round: the calm cut, and a tuner that fails open

**The channel-max gate on the calm cuts: 10.7x on Chainsaw, nothing on Violet.** Matched chains:

| Chainsaw 8 s | luma 4.0 | 0.00274% | 16 | 66.3% |
| Chainsaw 8 s | channel-max 0.07% | **0.02932%** | **22** | 66.3% |
| Violet 8 s | luma 4.0 | 0.04989% | 18 | 64.0% |
| Violet 8 s | channel-max 0.07% | **0.04989%** | **18** | 64.0% |

A share threshold accepts a small area with a large delta - the small-object-moves-far class the
block-max existed to catch - but the effect is content-specific: 10.7x on Chainsaw, byte-identical on
235 of 250 Violet frames. Margin falls from 36x to 3.4x on Chainsaw, both still passing. So the new
statistic fixes one class and gives up another; the gate needs both conditions.

An earlier reading of 0.08769% at peak 116 came from comparing chain 3 against a chain-2 baseline:
0 of 249 frames were identical between the renders, which is the signature of a different reuse set,
not of a different statistic.

**The auto tuner fails open with a share statistic.** Left ON, it wrote its choice (a level) into the
field read as a percentage: `threshold 3.0/255` means "3% of pixels", never exceeded, reuse 89/122

## Adversarial selection: the probe stops sampling the easy tail

`probe(..., adversarial=k)` scans the whole clip with the gate's own statistic (no model, one streaming
pass), ranks the reuses it would make, and renders the top-k.

|---|---|---|
| spread sample (16 triples) | 0.000% (no d2 pair) | 0.167% |
| adversarial top-13 | **0.110%** (control 0.083%) | 0.168% (control 0.077%) |
| full render | 0.08876% | 0.56613% |

On Chainsaw the probe goes from 26x under the render to 1.24x over it, and refuses a configuration the

sample already lands on the damage.

Two bugs, both caught by the self-check rather than by reading the code: `select=eq(n,index)` returns a
different frame than the stream index under B-frames (12 of 13 pairs had a measured share above the
gate's threshold), and without the check those pairs rendered a scene change as a composition error -
86.7% with an fp16 control of 86.3%, which is impossible on a pair the gate certified.

Records: `docs/wsp/verified/probe-adversarial.txt`.

## The forced flag was measuring itself

372 frames above the bar with only 340 reused is arithmetically impossible as composition: at least 32
*rendered* frames were failing, which is the body's own error. The cause was the flag:
`--int8_fit int8` skipped `_probe_int8_fit`, and that call is also what decodes the spread frames the
body calibrates on, so the body fell back to one frame.

| | calibration | worst | above the bar |
|---|---|---|---|
| before | 1 frame | 0.56613% | 372 |
| after | 24 frames | **0.12727%** | **3** |

All three survivors are *reused* frames, so it is composition after all - and the spread calibration is
exactly what `int8-split-calibration` demanded. The verdict stays fp16 (0.127 against 0.1), but the
margin and the mechanism are different, and the 0.566% reading is withdrawn.

**The 1.55x wall is withdrawn too.** With reuse r = 0.292, walls of 108.76 and 70.33 ms imply an engine
delta of 131.6 ms, which is impossible: the non-engine term changed. ffv1's cost depends on content
entropy and the quantised output encodes faster, so 1.55x is a codec effect, not inference. At a fixed
non-engine term the ratio would be 1.05x. The shipped int8 wall through libx264 is still unmeasured.

**And the factor is not multiplicative on Violet**: per-frame int8/fp16 over the 336 reused frames has
median 7.31, p90 89.8, max 746. The 1.18x on Chainsaw is a ratio of two maxima on one clip.

## The wall at a fixed cost, and the depth table from the render

`--output null` (rawvideo + null muxer) takes the encoder out of the loop: int8 **45.02 ms/frame**
against fp16 **50.33** at the same 17.3% reuse = **1.118x**, below the 1.49-1.505 engine ratio as Amdahl
requires. The retracted 1.55x was ffv1 encoding a quantised picture faster.

`RVE_DEBUG_DEPTH=1` logs every reuse with its depth, and the gate prints the histogram: 83 reuses,
{1: 41, 2: 36, 3: 6}. Joined with the sweep: worst HR 0.07546% at depth 1 (frame 475, the clip's worst),
0.02670 at d2, 0.00917 at d3. The replay's 78-reuse table is superseded by the render's own.

The probe's torch reference against the app's TRT engine: 0.0116% on one frame - small, and not the
cause of the control-vs-sweep gap, which is the pair set.

## The TRT engine in the probe

The earlier note said the engine was unreachable and that `_quantised_forward` had no engine equivalent.
The first half was wrong: `self.upscaleOption` exists before the probe runs, the engine builds, and it
reproduces the app's own ungated render at share 0.0%. `probe(..., engine_ref=...)` now uses it for the
fp16 reference in both paths.

| clip | measurement | torch fp16 | TRT engine |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chainsaw | adversarial | 0.110% | 0.109% |
| Chainsaw | control | 0.083% | 0.080% |
| Violet | depth-2 | 0.168% | **0.216%** |
| Violet | own defect | 0.044% | **0.081%** |

So the reference matters on Violet and not on Chainsaw, and the control is still ~6% above the sweep on
both - the residual gap is the pair set, not the reference.
