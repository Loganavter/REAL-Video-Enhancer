# The BD re-measurement: the gate's refusal is material, and the material was a re-encode

2026-11-18. The owner looked at the frames and the input turned out to be wrong: every content number in
this lane since 2026-11-15 was measured on h264 **4.038 / 3.982 Mbps** re-encodes that the prose called
"the BD remaster", not on the **37.715 / 39.552 Mbps** BDRemux. `docs/wsp/verified/sources-bd.txt` is the
input's own record; `harness.cli source-check` is the check that would have caught it on the first day.

This file is what the re-measurement found. The mechanism-level work - kernels, engines, launcher, graph,
peaks, the arithmetic of the ceilings, the three pipeline bugs - is untouched: it never read this
material. What follows is the content half only.

## Noise floors, same shot, only the encode changed

The auto gate's first act is to measure the material's own frame-to-frame delta and refuse reuse above
**1.5**. On the Violet shot the two files cover the same frames (the BD cut starts 9-11 frames earlier
because `-c copy` can only cut on a keyframe; frame `a[i]` of the re-encode matches `b[i+9..11]` at a
mean absolute difference of 0.23-0.25 levels):

| Violet, same shot | noise floor | what the gate did |
|---|---|---|
| 4.038 Mbps re-encode | **1.23** (recorded 2026-11-18 06:xx, re-run this session) | engaged: threshold 4.0, chain 4, **69.8%** of frames reused |
| 37.715 Mbps BD | **1.67** | **refused**: "noise floor 1.67 is above the 1.5 limit" |

The floor does not fall on the BD - it rises. The queue expected the opposite ("expect the noise floors
to fall ... compression noise was being counted as material noise"), and the direction matters here
because the refusal limit sits **between** the two numbers: the re-encode's smoothing is what let the
gate engage on this shot at all. A 4 Mbps encode that stays static inside a GOP makes consecutive
frames more alike; the BD keeps the grain that the gate reads as motion.

Chainsaw Man is the same story with a different ending: 0.66 (re-encode) against **1.00** (BD), both
below the limit, so the gate engages on either - 20.8% reused on the 20 s re-encode, **25.6%** on the
539-frame BD cut (chain 4, depths 1:64, 2:53, 3:12, 4:9). The battle-scene cut (`csm_battle.mkv`,
proved to be the same media as `bd_csm20s.mkv`) reads **22.58** and refuses, reproducing the value
already recorded in `gate-action-scenes.txt` to the digit - a control the new pipeline passes.

## Where the gate refuses, the render is bit-identical to the ungated one

The refusal is not "less reuse": it is no reuse, and the evidence is the identity row the protocol asks
for. Every pair below was compared frame by frame at 3840x2160, worst channel, over the whole clip:

| pair | frames | worst share | result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Violet BD gated vs ungated, 64px blocks | 494 | 0.00000% | **bit-identical** |
| Violet BD gated vs ungated, 32px blocks | 494 | 0.00000% | bit-identical |
| Chainsaw battle BD gated vs ungated | 503 | 0.00000% | bit-identical |
| **A/A** Violet BD, the same configuration rendered twice | 494 | 0.00000% | bit-identical |
| **A/A** Chainsaw battle BD rendered twice | 503 | 0.00000% | bit-identical |

So the defect class the gate was accused of - 0.49372% on 18 of 481 frames of the 4 Mbps Violet clip -
cannot occur on this material, because nothing is served from a reference. It is not that the error is
smaller; the code path is not taken.

## Chainsaw, the shot where the gate does engage

The calm Chainsaw cut reads a floor of **1.00** - under the limit - so the tuner certifies chain 4 and
reuses **138 of 539 frames (25.6%)**, depths 1:64, 2:53, 3:12, 4:9. The sweep against the ungated render:

| | |
|---|---|
| worst frame | **0.52379%** (frame 219) |
| frames above the 0.1% bar | **17 of 539** |
| worst 64 px block mean | 11.15 (same frame) |
| peak | 115 levels |

So the gate fails on BD exactly where it engages, and the magnitude is the recorded one: 0.494% on 18 of
481 frames on the re-encode, 0.524% on 17 of 539 here. The finding was right; the material was wrong.

What the eye sees on that frame (`EYE-CHECK-2026-11-18.md`) is a flash blob at the top of the range: the
gated render saturates it, the ungated keeps its shape, and the peak in that region is 16 levels - the
difference between clipped and nearly clipped. That is the class the luma projection cannot see, and it is
the reason the channel-max cell existed.

## The channel-max cell: passes the share, concentrates the damage

On Violet, where the luma gate refuses, a forced channel-max gate (0.07, chain 3) reuses **335 of 494
frames (67.8%)** and stays inside the bar: worst **0.07337%** (frame 257), **zero** frames above 0.1%.
But its worst 64 px block mean is **20.90** with a **peak of 142 levels** on a saturated edge (frame 179),
against the **7.57** block mean of the artifact the owner already rejected by eye. The eye check shows a
thin white crescent on a rim-light edge - the share says "0.056% of the frame", the block mean says
"concentrated", and the owner's verdict was the block mean's: the lane is shelved.

## int8 on BD: two orders of magnitude between two clips

| clip | int8, reuse off | frames above 0.1% |
|---|---|---|
| Violet BD | **0.53318%** (frame 29) | **356 / 494** |
| Chainsaw same-shot BD | **0.00339%** (frame 205) | **0 / 539** |

The recorded Violet number was 0.08876% with zero frames - on the re-encode. Per-tensor activation grids
are calibrated per clip, so "int8 is accurate" was never a statement about the engine; on one title it is
nearly exact and on another it is over the bar on 72% of frames. The failure is not a patch: the diff puts
the **line art** in white - int8 moves the ink, not the flat colour.

## The wall, with the sink out of the way

`--output null` (writer ~10 ms/frame), one session:

| clip | configuration | ms/frame | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Violet BD | fp16, reuse off | 60.15 | 1.00 |
| Violet BD | int8, reuse off | 52.60 | **1.14x** |
| Chainsaw same-shot | fp16, reuse off | 59.42 | 1.00 |
| Chainsaw same-shot | fp16 + luma gate | 47.94 | **1.24x** |
| Chainsaw same-shot | int8, reuse off | 62.42 | 0.95x |

So the gate's 1.24x is real on the calm shot and costs 0.52379% of pixels; int8 is 1.14x on Violet and
slightly slower than fp16 on Chainsaw.

## The decision

Frame skipping is **shelved and off by default** (owner, 2026-11-18): on a 1080p source the noise floor is
almost always above the 1.5 refusal limit, and at low contrast a small delta can be significant motion
rather than noise. `--temporal_reuse` is the opt-in flag; the engine half - 1.63x - is what ships.

## What this does to the conclusions

- **`gate-exceeds-acceptance-bar` is replaced.** It rested on 18/481 frames at 0.49372%, measured on
  the re-encode of a shot whose floor (1.23) sat below the refusal limit. On the BD of that shot the
  tuner refuses, and the render is the ungated render to the bit.
- **The gate's mechanism is not refuted** - on the calm Chainsaw shot it engages, certifies chain 4 and
  reuses a quarter of the frames, and the sweep above says what that costs in picture.
- **The flipped cell (channel-max block mean) loses its measured motive.** The 37.27 block mean against
  the rejected 7.57 was measured on the re-encode; the hold it justified is re-examined against the BD
  numbers below.
- **`int8-d2-owner-split`, `int8-live-verdicts` and `int8-peak-unmet` were measured on the re-encodes**
  (18 of the 124 contaminated records sit in `int8-d2-control.txt`). The probe's numbers on BD material
  are different in both directions: Violet refuses harder (composition d1 0.266%, d2 0.334%, peak 25,
  engine 0.082% on 7 pairs) while the Chainsaw battle cut *passes* (0.010%, peak 19 on 4 pairs) where the
  recorded verdict said both titles refuse.

## What is not covered

- One 20 s cut per shot. The gate's floor is a property of the calibration window, so a different
  opening can read differently; this session measures the window each clip actually has.
- The BD cuts declare **no colour matrix and no range**; the re-encodes declared `tv`. The app converts
  with `cv2.COLOR_YUV2RGB_I420` (BT.601 full range) while the project's ffmpeg simulators assume
  BT.709/tv. Inside a gated-vs-ungated delta that disagreement is common-mode; against the source it is
  not, and it is not resolved here.
- The frame counts differ (495/503 against 480/480), so a frame index from one cut does not name the
  same picture in the other.
- Every wall number in this lane was taken with a file sink that spends 80-120 ms/frame in the ffv1
  writer; only the `--output null` runs measure the render.
