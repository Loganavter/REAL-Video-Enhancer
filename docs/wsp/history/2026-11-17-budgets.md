## E29 phase 1: the L2 pyramid's arithmetic holds, and its ceiling is the compute floor, not the traffic

Measured 2026-11-17 by `harness/harness/scripts/pyramid_budget.py` - CPU arithmetic, no GPU job, so nothing here can
drift between sessions. The question E29 was written to answer: how many bytes must stay on-chip
between two body layers, does the 16-boundary sum fit L2, and what does that buy?

| | 1920x1080 | 3840x2160 |
|---|---|---|
| one frame tensor (64 ch fp16) | 253.1 MB | 1012.5 MB |
| reuse buffer, one boundary (2 rows x W x 64 ch x 2 B) | **480 KB** | **960 KB** |
| 16 boundaries | **7.5 MB** | **15.0 MB** |
| vs the card's **32 MiB** L2 | fits | fits |
| logical traffic (read + write per boundary) | 8.49 GB | 33.97 GB |
| DRAM time at 288 GB/s (measured effective) | 29.5 ms | 118.0 ms |
| compute floor, fp16 at the borrowed 74 TFLOP/s | **33.1 ms** | 132.2 ms |
| compute floor, int8 at the 91 TOPS the convs report | 26.9 ms | 107.5 ms |

The first half of the refutes clause **does not fire**: the buffer is 7.5 MB at 1080p and 15 MB at 4K,
both inside 32 MiB, so the 16-boundary sum stays resident. (The ledger's earlier figure of 0.49 MB per
boundary is the same arithmetic rounded.)

The second half is where the item changes shape. At 1080p the three candidate ceilings are:

| L2 absorbs | DRAM ms | roofline `max(compute, DRAM)` fp16 | as a speedup on the measured 56.8 ms |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0% | 29.5 | 33.1 | 1.72x |
| 1/3 (the ledger's assumed absorption) | 19.7 | **33.1** | **1.72x** |
| 100% | 0.0 | 33.1 | 1.72x |

Absorbing **all** the traffic changes the ceiling by nothing, because the fp16 compute floor (33.1 ms)
is already above the unabsorbed DRAM time (29.5 ms) - the plan is compute-bound at 1080p, not
traffic-bound, which is what the ncu counters said about the int8 block from the other direction
(51.2% tensor pipe, 59.4% DRAM). At 4K the DRAM time (118 ms) is **below** the compute floor (132.2 ms)
too, so the pyramid buys nothing there either.

What that means for the item: the pyramid is a way to approach the compute floor, and the compute floor
is 1.72x from the measured engine. The 2.5x-shaped hopes attached to it were traffic-shaped. And the
1/3 absorption is not a measurement of this graph - it is one kernel's ncu sector-hit rate applied to
sixteen boundaries, which is exactly the population substitution this ledger keeps withdrawing.

Records: `docs/wsp/verified/pyramid-budget.txt`. Phase 2 (a fused prototype) would have to beat
56.8 -> 33.1 ms with a schedule the builder cannot currently emit; the arithmetic does not justify
starting it ahead of the int8 lanes.

## E36 phase 1: the cone is 2.1-2.5x less work, and 0.08-0.27 ms per frame - the ratio is not the number

Measured 2026-11-17 by `harness/harness/scripts/cone_budget.py` (CPU arithmetic). The frame gate reuses or drops whole
frames; E36 asks what a **partially** changed frame would cost if only the changed region were
recomputed, with a halo that shrinks with depth: layer L (1..16) reads the changed region dilated by
`17-L` px, while a cylinder gives every layer the full 18 px receptive halo.

Control first, because a ratio without one is a convention: the ledger's own 1-D case (a 2 px band,
cylinder `16*38*L` against cone `304*L`) must read 2.00x and does.

| changed square | side | cylinder px | cone px | work ratio | cone share of a frame | work ms | launch floor | binding |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| r=5 | 11 | 35,344 | 13,904 | **2.54x** | 0.042% | 0.024 | 0.080 | launch |
| r=10 | 21 | 51,984 | 24,464 | **2.12x** | 0.074% | 0.042 | 0.080 | launch |
| r=20 | 41 | 94,864 | 55,184 | 1.72x | 0.166% | 0.094 | 0.080 | work |
| r=40 | 81 | 219,024 | 155,024 | 1.41x | 0.467% | 0.265 | 0.080 | work |

The ratios reproduce the review's 2.6x at r=5 and 2.16x at r=10 under an explicitly stated convention,
so the arithmetic survives contact.

The number that actually matters is the **last two columns**. Sixteen launches at ~5 us cost 0.08 ms,
and the GEMM work for r<=20 is *less than that*: the recompute is launch-bound, not work-bound. So the
prize is not "2.1x less work", it is **0.08-0.27 ms instead of 56.8 ms** on a frame the gate would
otherwise re-render in full - a 200-700x saving on the frames it applies to.

That is also why this is not adoptable yet, and the blocker is the same one the tile path died on: the
gate's veto is frame-level, and a cone that underestimates the changed region is wrong by construction.
A region-level certificate does not exist, and the seams between a recomputed cone and a reused
neighbourhood are exactly the artifact class that made tile reuse read 0.287% at 9.2 s.

Records: `docs/wsp/verified/cone-budget.txt`.
