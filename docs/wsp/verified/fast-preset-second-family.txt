# Fast on a SECOND model family: 1.23x, same shape of answer - and the card question stays open

Measured 2026-11-20 (local), RTX 4060 Ti, BD Violet 1080p -> 4x, null sink, reuse off, two arms alternating in
one session, 2 rounds. Records: `fast-preset.jsonl`; raw: `logs/fast-preset/animevideov3/`.

Row `rve-fast-preset-per-hardware-2026-11-18`. Its question: the Fast preset's speed-up and its picture cost
were measured on one card and one model, so is the wording - "the speed-up depends on the card" - backed by
anything, and should Fast be a default?

## What was measurable here, and what was not

- **A second model family: measurable, and measured.** `realesr-animevideov3` is a 64-channel Compact with a
  4x upscale, i.e. a different family that the int8 body serves.
- **A second card: not measurable.** This machine has one GPU. No number in this file speaks to another card,
  and pretending otherwise would be exactly the population-label error the lane has retracted ten times.

## The second family

| arm | round 1 | round 2 | median |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best (fp16 engine) | 78.88 | 77.55 | **78.22** |
| Fast (int8 body) | 63.67 | 63.64 | **63.66** |

**1.229x**, against 1.203-1.221x for the 2x model in the same session-family of runs. Two implementation notes
that matter for reading it:

- The int8 tail kernel **declined this model** - "the tail has 48 output channels and this kernel pads to 16"
  - and fell back to cuDNN, so the 1.23x is Fast *without* the tail gain the 2x model gets. The guard works on
  a real model family, which is the kind of thing a guard is for and which a unit test cannot show.
- The fused head kernel **did** apply (the app pads this model's head to 8 input channels exactly as it does
  the 2x one), so the head side of the comparison is like for like.

First-frame picture against the fp16 model on this family: **0.01599%** of pixels above 8 levels - the body's
own gross-error gate, on one frame, not an acceptance number.

## The decision

1. **Fast stays an opt-in.** `--quality_preset` defaults to `best`; nothing here argues for changing that,
   and the one published external result on this question cuts the other way: a fused INT8 GEMM that wins
   2.8-4.2x per GEMM on consumer Ampere loses to the native bf16/FP8 paths on A100 and B200
   (arXiv:2606.14598). "Int8 is faster" is a property of the card.
2. **The wording is updated to say what has now been measured**: two model families, ~1.2x end to end, the
   picture indistinguishable on the 2x model across whole clips, and the card caveat kept because it is the
   one axis that remains untested.

So the row closes as the wording getting *more* precise rather than as a re-derivation: the model axis is
answered (the speed-up survives a second family), and the card axis cannot be answered from this machine at
all. If a second GPU ever exists on this host, the acceptance is already written: the same one-frame, no-pipe
comparison (model vs int8 body vs fp16 engine) plus an interleaved wall A/B, and a decision on the default.

## Not covered

- One card. This is the row's own limit, and no reading of these numbers removes it.
- Two rounds (2 of 2), not three: the two arms differ by 14 ms, far outside the session's drift, and the
  question is a sign and a rough size rather than a new bar.
- The 4x family's picture is one frame's gross gate, not a render pair. A full-clip pair on that family is
  the follow-up if anyone wants Fast justified there at acceptance strength.
- `Archivist_AntiLines` (48 body channels) is a third family the int8 body **cannot** serve at all - the
  coverage boundary, stated in `Int8Body` and unchanged by anything here.
