# The depth-2 blocker has an owner: the gate on Chainsaw, the sample on Violet

Measured 2026-11-17 with the product's own probe (`backend/src/utils/Int8Probe.py`) through
`scripts/probe_int8_app.py`, one GPU job at a time under `/var/tmp/opencode/gpu.lock`, bundled
interpreter torch 2.9.0+cu130 - the same instrument the live lane used. Three sample sizes on the two
clips that lane ran.

The probe now prints, on the SAME pairs and at the SAME separation it reads the composition on, the fp16
reference against fp16. Only the renderer changes, so the gap between the two lines is the quantiser's
share of the depth-2 number and the rest is the gate's own reference. That control needed no int8 and
was named as the missing piece in `int8-composition-d2`; it was never run until now.

## The product's sample: 16 triples, 8 test pairs, 2-6 of them at depth 2

`RenderVideo._probe_int8_fit` calls `probe(...)` with the default `frames=16`, so this is the decision
the product actually makes.

| clip | d2 int8 | d2 fp16 | fp16 share of d2 | d1 int8 | d1 fp16 | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chainsaw Man 20 s | 0.2177% | **0.1910%** | 88% | 0.000072% | 0.000% | fp16 |
| Violet Evergarden 20 s | 0.1219% | 0.0769% | 63% | 0.0583% | 0.0271% | fp16 |

On Chainsaw the gate's own reference alone reads 0.191% against a 0.1% bar: the refusal survives a
renderer with no quantisation error at all. The quantiser adds 0.027 pp there.

## The estimator, not the material: three samples of the same two clips

Each `frames` value samples different times (`t = duration*(i+0.5)/count`), so these are three
populations, not one growing sample. The harness refuses to compare across them, and that refusal is
the finding.

| clip | sample | test pairs | d2 pairs | d2 int8 | d2 fp16 | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chainsaw | 16 | 8 | 2 | 0.2177% | 0.1910% | fp16 |
| Chainsaw | 24 | 12 | 4 | 0.1603% | 0.1389% | fp16 |
| Chainsaw | 32 | 16 | 5 | 0.1748% | 0.1580% | fp16 |
| Violet | 16 | 8 | 6 | 0.1219% | 0.0769% | fp16 |
| Violet | 24 | 12 | 7 | 0.0248% | 0.0131% | **int8** |
| Violet | 32 | 16 | 11 | 0.0265% | 0.0045% | **int8** |

Four things follow.

1. **Chainsaw's refusal is robust and it is the gate's.** The fp16 reference is above the bar in all
   three samples (0.1910 / 0.1389 / 0.1580), and the quantiser contributes 0.017-0.027 pp. The gate
   certifies a pair whose block-mean delta is <= 5 levels while its acceptance statistic is 1.4-1.9x
   the bar - the E25 gap, now measured on the gate's own reference with no int8 in the loop.
2. **Violet's refusal is a property of the 8-pair sample.** At 12 and 16 pairs the same probe passes,
   at 0.0248% and 0.0265%. A verdict that flips between 0.0265 and 0.1219 on a 0.1% bar is not a
   measurement of the material; the estimator's spread is the same order as the bar.
3. **The registry's suspicion was wrong in an instructive way.** `int8-composition-d2` asked whether one
   of the two numbers was "not the population it is labelled with". Both are. What differs between the
   titles is the *reference term's share*: 88% on Chainsaw, 63% on Violet - and on Chainsaw that share
   alone already fails the bar.
4. **The d2 statistic is thinner than the engine statistic.** It is the intersection of the test pairs
   with the pairs the gate would reuse, so on the product's sample Chainsaw's depth-2 number rests on
   **2 pairs** while its engine number rests on 8. The protocol asks 13-19 frames for an acceptance
   sample; the depth-2 subset never reaches it.

## Why a bigger sample was impossible before this run

The probe held `ref` and `got` for every pair: ~200 MB per decoded 4K triple, so 16 triples sat at
11.2 GiB and a 32-triple run died with `CUDA out of memory` on the first clip. The three statistics are
now taken inside the per-pair loop and the tensors are dropped, so the sample size is bounded by time
rather than by memory. That change is why the 24- and 32-triple rows exist at all.

## What this does not cover

- The gate serves "the last kept frame no older than `max_chain`", and whether a k-deep reference exists
  depends on the chain state and the pixel veto (E25). The probe approximates it with "both block-mean
  steps pass". The control holds that approximation fixed, so it measures the quantiser's share of the
  *probe's* depth-2 number, not of the gate's actual reuse.
- The pair order is mirrored. `_read_pairs` returns three frames in time order, and the probe renders
  index 0 against references 1 and 2 - so the render is the EARLIEST frame of the triple, while the gate
  only ever reuses a past frame. The separation is the same two steps; the direction is not what the
  gate does. Recorded here rather than silently changed, because changing it changes every d2 number
  above.
- The fp16 reference is the torch fp16 model, not the TensorRT fp16 engine the app falls back to.
