# What the peak actually is: 17 levels on one pixel, in slivers along ink lines - and zero pixels over 20

Measured 2026-11-20 (local), BD Violet, seven spread frames held out of the eight calibration frames, the
SHIPPED int8 default (fused head kernel + 16 int8 hops + int8 tail kernel) against the model's own fp16 output,
on the pre-encode 4K tensor.
Records: `peak-look.jsonl`; raw: `logs/peak-look/frames.json`; crops: `logs/peak-look/*.png`.

Why this exists: `bar_int8_peak` = 20 levels carries a PENDING OWNER DECISION note, and the number that note
argues about (a 29-55 range) is from the int8 kernel FAMILY as measured on 2026-11-17, not from what ships
today. No one had looked at the shipped default's worst pixel - the two adopted changes were recorded as
DELTAS between int8 configurations (6 and 13 levels), which is a different quantity.

| 400 | 13.4 | 0.00549% | **0** | 0 | 9 px (of 318) |
| 460 | 13.1 | 0.00310% | **0** | 0 | 4 px (of 207) |

**The shipped default does not reach 20 levels anywhere, on any of the seven frames.** Its worst pixel is
16.9, and there is not a single pixel over 20 in 7 x 8.3 M pixels. Both bars are met: share 0.01461% against
0.1%, peak 16.9 against 20.

## What it looks like - and where it lives

Two crops were rendered as four panels each (`peak_look.py`: reference | shipped default | difference x8 |
the >8 mask): `peak_frame0169_crop_y2134_x2929_peak17_blob12.png` and
`peak_frame0274_crop_y38_x468_peak15_blob19.png`. Read them in that order - the first pair look identical, and
the mask panel is where the story is.

- **The errors are on ink lines, not in flat areas.** The mask panel of frame 274 shows a thin red sliver
  tracking a single vertical contour, exactly the place the int8 rounding has something to lose: a
  high-contrast edge between a dark line and a flat field.
- **They are slivers, not patches.** The largest connected run of >8-level pixels on any frame is **19 px**,
  and the typical frame's largest is 4-12 px. The rest are single pixels: 207-768 separate runs per frame.
  At 4K, 19 pixels is a 5x4 speck on a 3840x2160 canvas.
- **The 8x difference panel is a haze over the whole crop**, which is why gain panels mislead: at x8, a
  1-2 level rounding difference is visible everywhere. The mask is the honest panel - it shows only what
  crosses the 8-level threshold.

## What this does to the decision

The bar's question was "is a peak of 29-55 acceptable, or is 20 right?". On the shipped path the question does
not arise: **the default is inside 20 by 3 levels**, and the 29-55 range belongs to configurations that are not
shipped (the older epilogue/three-accumulator variants the ledger measured). So:

- `bar_int8_peak` **stays at 20**, and the shipped default **meets** it. No threshold needs inventing and no
  product claim needs an "out of spec on the peak axis" label.
- The 29-55 family range remains a true statement about those configurations; it is not a statement about the
  default, and quoting it as one would be the population error this lane keeps paying for.
- What is still a human judgement, and stays one: whether a 17-level speck on an ink contour at 4K is
  acceptable. The measurement can say it is invisible at x1 and 19 px at its largest; it cannot say whether
  anyone cares.

## Not covered

- The pre-encode 4K tensor, not the delivered stream. The colour pipe alone moves 0.21% of pixels past the
  8-level bar on 4:2:0 material (`int8-scales-bd`), which is exactly why render pairs are the acceptance
  instrument and this is an attribution instrument: the numbers here are the body's own error, not the
  product's.
- Seven frames of one clip, one model, one card. The largest runs are a property of the material's ink lines.
- The chunks were chosen as the largest 8-level component of each frame, which biases them toward the worst
  case - deliberately: a test that shows the typical case would be a sales document.
