# Beacon's closed-form scale loses to the swept K by 2000x, and the reason is clipping

Measured 2026-11-20 on `2x-Futsuu-Anime(1).pth`, 1920x1080, calibrated on 8 frames, measured on 3 held-out frames
from the same two bluray cuts. Records `int8-beacon-scale.jsonl` (BK001-BK014), raw
`logs/beacon/beacon.json`, `logs/beacon/beacon-clipping.json`, probe `harness/scripts/beacon_scale_probe.py`.

The question came out of the prior-art pass (`PRIOR-ART-STARTUP-2026-11-20.md`): Beacon (arXiv:2508.20293) says the
scale does not have to be searched - pick the integer codes on an unscaled grid, then take the least-squares
optimal scale, `c = <Xw,Xq>/||Xq||²` per channel. Our grid clamp K *is* a scale search: two candidate builds, six
frames, a render to decide. If the closed form works, the sweep and its start-up guard disappear.

## The result

| policy | csm t=10 | violet t=15 | violet t=5 | mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| K=2 (shipped default) | 0.00000% | 0.00016% | 0.00038% | **0.00018%** |
| K=3 | 0.00000% | 0.00092% | 0.00279% | 0.00124% |
| K=4 | 0.00000% | 0.00353% | 0.00397% | 0.00250% |
| **fitted (Beacon)** | 0.00000% | **0.62345%** | **0.47663%** | **0.36669%** |

Share of pixels with channel-max |delta| above 8 levels against the fp16 model. The fitted grids are **2000x
worse** than K=2 on the two frames that discriminate, and identical on the one that does not (the clean csm frame,
where every policy reads 0).

## Why: the closed form does not model clipping

The fit minimises `||y - c·q||²` over the codes, which is dominated by the bulk of the distribution. A grid that is
too tight improves the bulk and clips the tail - and our grid is per-channel *activation* scale, so a tight grid
saturates at ±127. Measured per hop on the failing frame:

| | K=2 | fitted |
|---|---|---|
| grid, mean over channels, last hop | 0.04240 | 0.01509 (**2.81x tighter**) |
| codes at the int8 rail, last hop | 0.000% | **31.25%** |
| codes at the rail, hop 14 | 0.000% | 12.50% |
| codes at the rail, hop 11 | 0.000% | 3.13% |

So the fitted scale is 1.5-2.8x tighter than the K=2 grid and the late hops saturate up to a third of their
channels. The sweep's winner is not a better least-squares solution - it is the one that **does not clip**, and the
closed form cannot know that because the L2 objective has no clipping term.

This is not an argument against Beacon in its own setting: there the grid is asymmetric min-max per column and the
scale is fitted *after* rounding, so clipping is bounded by the zero point. Here the scale is the clipping bound
itself, and a least-squares fit to it is the wrong estimator.

## What was NOT tried, and why it is not the reason for the verdict

- **The error-correction variant.** Beacon's EC form fits against the partially quantised model's inputs, which
  would need per-hop capture inside the body. It corrects the *input distribution* mismatch, not the clipping term -
  the failing hops saturate at 12-31% of channels, and no input correction recovers codes that hit the rail.
- **A clipping-aware fit** (minimise L2 subject to a saturation budget) is the obvious repair, and it is a different
  method: it needs a saturation target, which is a hyper-parameter we would then have to choose - which is the
  sweep again, with a proxy in the middle.
- **The ladder's 22 models.** Not run: one model, three frames, and a 2000x gap with an identified mechanism. If the
  gap were within 2x, the ladder would be the next step; at this size it would only re-measure the same failure.

## Verdict

Row `rve-beacon-scale-2026-11-20` closes as **refuted**: the closed-form scale does not replace the sweep, and the
mechanism is that the least-squares objective ignores the clipping that our per-channel activation grid exists to
control. The sweep stays, at its 3 s, with the K=2-clean skip.
