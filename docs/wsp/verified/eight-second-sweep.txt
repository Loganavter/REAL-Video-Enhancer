# The 8 s numbers were not under-sampled - so the 20 s failure is content, not sampling

The shipping gate numbers were all worst-of-13-to-19-frames. The question this answers: would a full
sweep of the same cuts have found a frame the sample missed, the way the 20 s clip did.

Both cuts were re-made from the remastering library at 5:00 (`-ss 300 -t 9.2 -c copy`, no re-encode, so
the source noise is the same) and rendered twice each, shipped defaults against
`--no_temporal_reuse`, in the lossless container. The conditions reproduce the ledger: noise floor
**0.95** and **1.32**, reuse **66.3%** and **64.0%** against the ledger's 66.4% and 64.8%.

| cut | full sweep | 15-frame sample | under-report | frames above 0.1% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chainsaw Man 8 s | **0.00274%** (frame 182, peak 16) | 0.00069% | 4.0x | 0 |
| Violet Evergarden 8 s | **0.04989%** (frame 230, peak 18) | 0.04496% | 1.1x | 0 |

So the sample does under-report on Chainsaw by 4x - and the number it under-reports is 0.0027% against a
0.1% bar, i.e. 36x of headroom. On Violet the sample was already within 1.1x and the sweep finds
0.0499%, half the ledger's 0.096% and still inside the bar with 2x of margin.

**The 20 s clip's 0.494% is therefore not a sampling artifact.** It is a different segment of the
material: the 8 s cut at 5:00 reuses 66% of its frames at a noise floor of 0.95, while the 20 s clip
reuses 20.8% at 0.66 and puts 17 of its 18 bad frames in the last 3.5 seconds. The gate is fine on a
calm cut and fails on an action sequence, and no sample size inside the calm cut would have shown it.

This closes the "99x portability failure" reading for good: the shipping numbers were not measured badly,
they were measured on different content.
