# Violet with int8 forced: 0.566%, 372 frames above the bar - the probe was right to refuse

Measured 2026-11-18 by forcing the body past the probe (`--int8_fit int8`, added for measurement only)
on the same clip, same channel-max gate at 0.07%, same 70.8% reuse.

| | fp16 + channel-max | int8 + channel-max |
|---|---|---|
| worst frame | 0.07345% (227) | **0.56613%** (280) |
| frames above 0.1% | 0 | **372 of 480** |

So the refusal on Violet is not a sampling artifact: the body damages that material badly, and the
probe's 0.108% was, if anything, an understatement.

## The probe under-reports on both clips

| clip | probe says | the render says |
|---|---|---|
| Chainsaw | 0.000% (d1, no depth-2 pair) | 0.08876% |
| Violet | 0.108% (d1) | **0.56613%** |

On Chainsaw the direction was right and the verdict matches; on Violet the direction was right and the
margin was wrong by 5x. The probe is systematically optimistic against the render - the class-2 failure
this lane retracted once already - so its numbers should be read as a screen, not as a certificate.

## The wall, and the cost of the new statistic

Same clip, same gate, same reuse, both written lossless: int8 70.33 ms/frame against fp16 108.76, i.e.
**1.55x**, consistent with the 1.49-1.505x the lane measured under the encoder. These walls include
ffv1 encoding and are not the shipped wall (the shipped Violet run reads 35.12 ms through libx264).

The gate's own entry cost: **4.42 ms** per frame with channel-max (2.49 of it the tensor conversion),
against **5.39 ms** (3.34) for the shipped luma gate on the same clip in the lane's own log. The new
statistic is not more expensive than the old one, which contradicts the assumption that a full
three-channel diff must cost more than a pooled luma.
