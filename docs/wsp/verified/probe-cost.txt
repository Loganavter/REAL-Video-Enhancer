# Where the probe's 64.6 s goes

Measured 2026-11-17 by timing each phase of `Int8Probe.probe` on the same clip and interpreter, with a
`torch.cuda.synchronize()` after each. The question it answers: can the depth-2 sample be raised to the
protocol's 13-19 without losing the lane's break-even, which sits at 16.2 min of video.

| phase | s | share |
|---|---|---|
| decode (`_read_pairs`, 16 triples by ffmpeg seek) | 2.92 | 5.7% |
| model load and upload | 0.15 | 0.3% |
| calibrate (24 frames, a hook per conv and per PReLU) | 21.23 | 41.7% |
| forwards (48 fp16 + 24 `_quantised_forward`) | 19.05 | 37.5% |
| the depth-2 control's extra pass (48 fp16) | 7.52 | 14.8% |
| **total** | **50.87** | |

Decode is not the cost. Calibration is the largest single term and the forwards are second, so a
32-triple sample roughly doubles 80% of the probe: ~100 s against ~51, and the break-even moves from
16.2 to about 32 min of video. The protocol's sample is therefore not free, and the place to buy it back
is calibration - 21.2 s for 24 frames is 0.88 s per frame against 0.157 s for a plain fp16 forward, so
the hooks, not the model, are paying.
