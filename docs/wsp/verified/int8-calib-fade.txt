# A clip that opens on black: pure sequential calibration reads 4.13%, the hybrid reads 0.038%

Measured 2026-11-20 on a real clip, not a synthetic one: the first 25 s of `Interstellar.2014.IMAX.EDITION.720p`,
whose first three seconds are **black** (mean luma 3.5 of 255 over 72 frames), cut at 1280x720. Records
`int8-calib-fade.jsonl` (FD001-FD004), raw `logs/calib-fade/`.

The row exists because the sequential decode (`int8-calib-seq.txt`) was shipped on one clip whose head is ordinary
content - BD Violet cannot see the failure mode it introduces: a sequential sample of the first 48 frames gives the
grids a **lower ceiling** than the clip contains, and a low ceiling clips later. The 2026-11-18 crop page measured
the same mechanism between one frame and sixteen (0.116% against 0.006%).

## The failure

`gate_sweep --reference-kind config`, spread decode as the reference:

| arm | worst share | frames above 0.1% | peak |
|---|---|---|---|
| **pure sequential**, first 48 frames | **4.13308%** (frame 109) | **184 of 266** | **124** |
| **hybrid**, 12 sequential triples + 4 spread timestamps | **0.03777%** (frame 101) | 0 | 23 |

The pure-sequential arm is not a small regression: two thirds of the clip is above the bar, and the peak is 124
levels. That is a visibly damaged picture, and it is what "the head of the clip is black" does to a calibration
that only looks at the head.

## The fix, and why it is the hybrid

A spread sample is expensive (16 seeks, 4.66 s) and a sequential one is blind to the head (0.63 s). The hybrid
keeps most of the speed and removes the blindness: **12 triples read sequentially + 4 triples at spread
timestamps**, so the sample always contains the middle of the clip. Cost is about 1.6 s of decode instead of 4.66.

| clip | hybrid against full spread |
|---|---|
| Interstellar cut (opens black, 266 frames) | **0.03777%**, 0 frames above the bar |
| BD Violet (494 frames) | **0.00035%**, 0 frames above the bar |

Start-up with the hybrid: **6.05 s** (FD004), against 4.95 s for the pure sequential and 9.79 s for the full
spread. So the safety costs 1.1 s, and the session's start-up still went 33.15 -> 6.05 s.

## What this says about the earlier row

`int8-calib-seq.txt` shipped `RVE_INT8_CALIB_SEQUENTIAL=1` with a pure sequential sample and named this risk in
prose. The prose was right and the default was wrong: a clip whose head is black is not an edge case (Interstellar
opens on black, and so do most studio logos), so the risk had to be measured before the default moved, not after.
The knob now means the hybrid, `RVE_INT8_CALIB_SPREAD=4` sets how many spread timestamps it keeps, and `=0` gives
the pure sequential sample that this page refutes.

## What is NOT claimed

- **Two clips.** One with a black head, one ordinary. Enough to show the failure and that the hybrid survives it,
  not enough to set the spread count for every kind of head (a 30 s logo sequence would want more than 4).
- **The spread count was not swept.** 4 is the smallest number that covers the middle of a clip in four places.

## Reproduce

```bash
ffmpeg -i <film> -t 25 -c:v libx264 -crf 12 -pix_fmt yuv420p inter25s.mkv     # the head must be kept
ARMS="spread seq hybrid" ARM_ENV_SPREAD="RVE_INT8_CALIB_SEQUENTIAL=0" \
    ARM_ENV_SEQ="RVE_INT8_CALIB_SEQUENTIAL=1 RVE_INT8_CALIB_SPREAD=0" \
    ARM_ENV_HYBRID="RVE_INT8_CALIB_SEQUENTIAL=1" SINK=ffv1 ROUNDS=1 CLIPS="inter25s.mkv" scripts/wall_ab.sh
PYTHONPATH=. python3 harness/scripts/gate_sweep.py <arm>.mkv <spread>.mkv --reference-kind config --out pair.json
PYTHONPATH=. python3 harness/scripts/calib_fade_records.py
PYTHONPATH=. python3 -m harness.cli check docs/wsp/verified/int8-calib-fade.txt \
    --records docs/wsp/verified/records/int8-calib-fade.jsonl --bars docs/claims-bars.json
```
