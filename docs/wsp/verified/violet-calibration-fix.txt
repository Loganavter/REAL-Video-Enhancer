# Violet's 0.566% was the forced flag measuring itself

The arithmetic that exposed it: 372 frames above the bar but only 340 reused, so at least 32 *rendered*
frames were above the bar - the body's own error, not composition. That is not a composition failure,
and a probe that only looks at reuse pairs cannot see it.

Cause: `--int8_fit int8` skipped `_probe_int8_fit`, and that function is also what decodes the spread
frames the body calibrates on. `UpscaleTorch` falls back to `frame` (one frame) when `int8Frames` is
None, so the forced run calibrated on the first frame:

```
BEFORE:  int8 body self-certification 0.00000%  -> worst 0.56613%, 372 of 480 above the bar
AFTER:   int8 calibration frames handed to the body: 24
         int8 fit FORCED to int8; the probe said fp16
         worst 0.12727% (frame 227), 3 of 480 above the bar
```

The verdict is still fp16 (0.127% against a 0.1% bar), but the margin and the mechanism are different:
three frames, all three **reused**, i.e. composition after all. The spread calibration is what the
lane's own `int8-split-calibration` verdict demanded; the flag was bypassing it.

## The multiplicative factor does not hold on this material

Per-frame int8/fp16 ratio over the 336 reused frames: **median 7.31, p90 89.8, max 746**. The 1.18x
quoted for Chainsaw is a ratio of two maxima on one clip, not a transferable factor; on Violet the
increment is not multiplicative at all.

## What this retracts

The earlier reading of 0.56613% and its 372 frames is withdrawn, together with the conclusion drawn
from it ("the body damages Violet badly"). What survives is: int8 on Violet reads 0.127% with three
composition frames over the bar, and the probe's refusal is correct.
