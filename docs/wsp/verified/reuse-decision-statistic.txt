# Reuse reopened, first measurement: a motion-compensated residual ranks the damaged reuses better than the block mean

Measured 2026-11-19 (local) after the owner reopened the lane (`reuse-gate-reopened`), on the RTX 4060 Ti,
the app's own renderer (`backend/rve-backend.py`, ffv1 sink, 2x Futsuu, 1920x1080 -> 3840x2160). Records:
`records/reuse-decision-statistic.jsonl`; instrument: `scripts/reuse_stats.py`, damage from
`scripts/gate_sweep.py`.

## Why this population and not the old one

The failure the lane was closed on was measured on `bd_csm_same.mkv` (539 frames, 17 frames above the bar).
That file is **not reproducible**: `sources.yaml` records it as `cut: {mode: not_proved}` - no packet
subsequence proof - and it lived in `/tmp`. So the labelled population had to be rebuilt on a cut whose
provenance holds.

A scan of eight 8 s `-c copy` cuts of the same BDRemux (Chainsaw Man ep.01) found three where the auto gate
engages: 1040 s (noise floor 0.87, 69% reuse), 340 s (0.93), 900 s (1.00). The other five refuse
(floors 3.9-51.5), including the 19:40 action segment (22.6). Rendered gated against ungated at 1040 s:

| cut | frames | worst share | frames above 0.1% |
|---|---|---|---|
| 900 s | 220 | 0.00623% | 0 |
| 340 s | 202 | 0.00181% | 0 |
| **1040 s** | **205** | **0.44121% (f122)** | **37** |

So the reopened lane starts from a *provable* failure: at 1040 s the gate reuses 130 of 205 frames (63.4%,
depth histogram {1: 68, 2: 62}) and **37 of those 130 reuses are past the acceptance bar** - 28% of its own
decisions. The bad frames are not scattered: the first is frame 110 and all 37 sit in the last ~95 frames,
i.e. the segment changes character and the statistic does not follow.

## Which per-frame statistic ranks those 37 frames?

Every candidate is computed on the same pairs, against the same t-1 reference, joined to the per-frame damage
from the gated-vs-ungated pair; AUC over the **130 reused frames only** (37 positives, 93 negatives), because
a rendered frame has no reuse damage by construction and would only dilute the test.

| statistic | AUC | median, damaged | median, clean |
|---|---|---|---|
| `block_maxabs` - the shipped gate: max over 32 px blocks of mean(|cur - prev|) | **0.844** | 3.36 | 1.13 |
| `block_signed` - the retired |mean( cur) - mean(prev)| | 0.723 | 2.28 | 0.60 |
| `mctf_p99` - DIS flow, warp, p99 of the residual | 0.855 | 5.31 | 2.00 |
| `mctf_mean` - the same residual, mean | **0.906** | 0.73 | 0.54 |
| `mctf_share` - residual above 3 x MAD-sigma, share | **0.384** | 5.24 | 11.15 |

Two results, one positive and one negative:

- **Motion compensation helps, modestly**: the mean of the motion-compensated residual ranks the damaged
  reuses at AUC 0.906 against the shipped statistic's 0.844, and the retired signed block mean is worst of
  the three block variants (0.723). The direction the owner described - the decision should be made under
  motion, not on raw intensity - is supported.
- **Normalising by the noise floor makes it worse than chance** (0.384): on flat regions the residual and its
  MAD-sigma are both tiny, so a small motion produces a large share. The "share above 3 sigma" form of the
  MCTF statistic is a trap and must not be shipped as the noise-aware variant.

## What this does and does not establish

- Establishes: on a provable cut, a motion-compensated residual is a measurably better *ranker* of bad reuses
  than the block mean (0.906 against 0.844, 130 reused frames, 37 positives), and the noise-normalised share
  is not.
- Does NOT establish: a threshold. A hold-out split is impossible on this clip - all 37 positives fall in the
  second temporal half (0 of 65 positives in the first), so a threshold calibrated here is a fit. The segment
  character change is itself the finding: the failure is a property of a stretch of frames, not of the clip.
- Does NOT establish: the temporal-texture criterion. The 0.1% share cannot see a frozen grain, which is why
  the channel-max cell passed the share and was rejected by eye; nothing here addresses that half.
- Scope: one episode, one cut, one model, fp16 engine, t-1 reference where the gate serves a chain of up to 2.



## The hold-out: the ranking gain does not transfer

The 1040 s row cannot calibrate a threshold (all 37 positives are in its second half), so a second failure was
found by the gate's own behaviour and only then measured: ten more 8 s `-c copy` windows of the same episode,
one render to null each to read the auto gate's noise floor. Seven engaged (offsets 120, 240, 400, 560, 700,
960, 1360 s; floors 0.35-1.48), three refused (820, 1120, 1240 s; floors 3.45-31.55). Of the four engaging
windows rendered as a lossless pair, one fails: **960 s - 200 frames, 123 reused, 7 above the 0.1% bar
(worst 0.52019% at frame 56)**; 240, 400 and 560 s are clean (0.008%, 0.002%, 0.004%).

Threshold picked on 1040 s by Youden's J over its reused frames, applied unchanged to 960 s:

| statistic | threshold (1040 s) | AUC, 1040 s | **AUC, 960 s** | caught, 960 s | false alarms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `block_maxabs` (shipped) | 2.172 levels | 0.844 | **0.994** | 3 / 7 | 0 / 116 |
| `mctf_mean` | 0.498 levels | 0.906 | **0.489** | 3 / 7 | 5 / 116 (4 per 100 clean) |

**The motion-compensated residual's advantage does not survive the hold-out.** On the clip it was measured on
it ranks better (0.906 against 0.844); on the held-out clip it collapses to chance (0.489) while the shipped
block statistic is nearly perfect (0.994) - and at its own 1040 s threshold the MCTF mean buys five false
alarms where the block statistic has none for the same three catches.

That is the honest state of the first hypothesis: "the decision should be made under motion" is not supported
as a general fix, and the 1040 s ranking was a property of that segment. What the two clips do say together is
that the failure class is *not stationary*: the statistic that ranks one segment's damage perfectly ranks the
other's at chance, in both directions. A decision variable for this artifact has to be evaluated per segment,
which is a stronger claim than either statistic makes.





## Correction, 2026-11-19 (late): the offline statistics used t-1, not the chain reference

Every AUC above was computed against `t-1`, while the gate serves the last KEPT frame, up to two back. An
outside review named that as an approximation that breaks portability, and it was right: recomputed against the
reference the gate actually served (`RVE_DEBUG_DEPTH` gives the depth of every reuse), the numbers change.

| statistic | clip | AUC vs t-1 | **AUC vs the served reference (t-k)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| `block_maxabs` (shipped) | 1040 s | 0.844 | **0.991** |
| `block_maxabs` (shipped) | 960 s (held out) | 0.994 | **0.988** |
| `mctf_mean` | 1040 s | 0.906 | 0.972 |
| `mctf_mean` | 960 s (held out) | 0.489 | **0.488** |

Two things follow, and the first one withdraws a claim made earlier in this file:

1. **"The failure class is non-stationary" was substantially an artefact of our own approximation.** Against the
   served reference the shipped statistic ranks the damaged reuses at 0.99 on BOTH clips (0.991 and 0.988), so
   there is no ranking non-stationarity to explain. The 0.844/0.994 spread was t-1 against t-k.
2. **The motion-compensated refutation survives the correction**: `mctf_mean` is 0.972 on 1040 s and 0.488 on
   960 s with the correct reference, i.e. it still collapses on the held-out clip. The mechanism is measurable
   and is the review's: on 960 s the seven damaged reuses sit at a mean flow magnitude of 2.26 against 0.48 on
   the clean ones (coherent motion, which warping removes), while on 1040 s the damaged reuses are at 0.057
   against 0.266 (local redraw at almost no global motion). A warp-blind statistic is blind exactly where the
   skip is blind.

So the state after the correction is narrower and more useful: the shipped statistic is a good RANKER (0.99),
what is missing is (a) a certification rule that turns a ranker into a threshold - it currently lets 37 of 130
reuses through - and (b) any criterion at all for the frozen texture, which 82 of those 130 pass.

## Reuse with a correction, the denoise prototype, and the trade-off between the two instruments

**Retraction first.** The first version of this section reported the correction at 0.26648% with texture 0.989.
That was an instrument bug: the frame reader asked ffmpeg for rgb24 but did not set `-pix_fmt`, so rawvideo
emitted yuv420p and every `read()` consumed two frames - the three streams were misaligned and the numbers
described nothing. Fixed in `scripts/reuse_residual.py` and `scripts/reuse_regrain.py`; everything below is on
the corrected reader. (Second instrument bug of this lane in two days; the pattern is the same - the reader was
trusted because the pipeline ran.)

On the same 130 reused frames, output = gated frame + `gain * nearest-2x(LR_t - LR_ref)`:

| gain | worst share | frames over 0.1% | texture ratio | frames frozen |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (verbatim skip) | 0.44121% | 37 / 130 | 0.000 | 91.5% |
| 1.0 | **0.09329%** | **0 / 130** | 0.388 | 91.5% |
| 2.0 | 0.67232% | 51 / 130 | 0.775 | 31.5% |
| 2.58 | 3.11501% | 117 / 130 | **1.000** | **0%** |

So the two objectives are the two ends of one knob. At unit gain the structural class is fixed completely -
**zero of 130 reuses over the bar**, against 37 for the verbatim skip - while the texture stays at 0.388,
which is about `1/alpha` with the net's noise gain `alpha` ~ 2.04 measured earlier: the correction adds the
fresh noise at 1x where the net adds its own response at ~2x. Turning the gain up to restore the texture
restores the pixel damage with it.

**The review's architecture, measured.** A motion-rejected 3-tap bilateral filter on LR (`scripts/reuse_denoise.py`,
tau 4, 2.03 levels of grain removed), the gate on the clean plate, then the removed grain re-added on ALL frames
(`scripts/reuse_regrain.py`):

| plate | reuse | worst share | frames over 0.1% | texture ratio | frozen |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| noisy (ships) | 63.4% | 0.44121% | 37 / 130 | 0.000 | 91.5% |
| denoised | **66.2%** | **0.79771%** | **42 / 204** | **0.9738** | **0%** |

The texture half is solved - 0.974 against the fresh render, nothing frozen - and the pixel half gets *worse*:
the clean plate lowers the noise floor (0.65 against 0.87), so the gate engages on more frames and its
structural errors are now un-masked. 42 of 204 frames over the bar, against 37 of 130 for the verbatim skip.


### The certification rule, simulated

The same ranker, used as a rule: skip when `block_maxabs` (against the served reference) exceeds the threshold
picked on 1040 s (2.172 levels), and count a violation when a skipped frame is past the 0.1% bar.

| clip | skipped | violations | rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1040 s (calibration) | 130 | 37 | **28.46%** |
| 960 s (held out) | 123 | 6 | **4.88%** |

So the rule is not a certificate: at the calibrated threshold the *calibration* clip itself violates on 28% of
its skips, and the held-out clip on 5%. A per-chunk audit controller (10% of each 20-reuse chunk rendered and
scored, threshold moved 5% up or 2% down by the audited rate) does not converge on this data: with two audited
frames per chunk the estimate is too noisy to steer, and the threshold drifts up (2.172 -> 2.485) while the
violations stay where they were. The arithmetic above says why - a sample of 18 cannot bound a 0.77% rate.

@claim RU023 28.46 % "reused frames [b7010cf3b0acf799] n=130" measured
@claim RU024 4.88 % "reused frames [8634db61097bc953] n=123" measured

**Why the certification cannot be bought cheaply.** A per-shot audit of the frames the gate wanted to skip is
the natural way to certify a threshold without labels. With zero violations in `n` audited reuses, the 95%
upper bound on the violation rate is `1 - 0.05^(1/n)`: 45% at n=5, 26% at n=10, 5.8% at n=50, 2.3% at n=130,
and **0.77% (one in 130) needs n=388**. Certifying the bar this lane actually uses - zero frames above 0.1% -
costs more audits than there are reuses to audit, so an audit can only certify a *rate*, and choosing a rate
is a policy decision, not a measurement.

## The published correction, done as published (NEMO Eq. 2)

NEMO reconstructs a non-anchor block as the *warped* cached SR plus the bilinearly upscaled codec residual; their
ablation prices the motion vector at 9.9 dB and the residual at 1.6 dB. Our earlier "residual reuse" omitted the
warp, i.e. the term worth 9.9 dB. Done the published way with DIS flow standing in for the codec's motion vectors
(`scripts/reuse_warp.py`): `out = remap(cached_SR, 2u) + bilinear_up(LR_t - warp(LR_ref, u))`.

| correction, gain 1.0 | worst share | frames over 0.1% | texture ratio | frozen |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| verbatim skip | 0.44121% | 37 / 130 | 0.000 | 91.5% |
| residual, NOT warped | **0.09329%** | **0 / 130** | 0.388 | 91.5% |
| **warped + residual (NEMO-style)** | 0.34400% | 6 / 130 | **0.838** | **0%** |
| warped + residual at gain 2.04 | 1.02387% | 46 / 130 | 1.348 | 0% |

The warp buys the texture back at unit gain (0.838 against 0.388) because remapping the cached SR moves its noise
too - the temporal change no longer depends on the correction's amplitude. It costs six frames over the bar,
against zero for the non-warped variant: those are where a DIS flow stands in badly for a real codec motion
vector (occlusion, disocclusion, a vector field that does not exist in the stream we took the frames from). At
gain 2.04 both instruments go wrong at once (46 frames, texture 1.348), so the texture is not the reason to raise
the gain in the warped variant.

So the two best configurations are still on opposite sides: `0 / 130 + 0.388` non-warped and
`6 / 130 + 0.838` warped. Neither is a pass, and the gap between them is the flow's quality rather than the
correction's algebra.

## The Palantir error DAG as a predictor: 0.776 on its own clip, 0.180 held out

Palantir predicts the SR error of a patch as `TC + sum(w * err_ref)`, with the texture term (`TC`) estimated from
the LR video alone - a predictor that needs no network run, which is what a certificate would need. Built in
`scripts/reuse_dag.py` with `TC` = block mean of `|upsample(downsample(LR)) - LR|`, one flow-mapped reference block
per block (their three-reference case does not exist in our material) and rendered frames treated as anchors
(`err = 0`):

| clip | reused | positives | AUC of the predicted error |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1040 s | 130 | 37 | 0.776 |
| 960 s, held out | 123 | 7 | **0.180** |

So the predictor is worse than the pairwise statistic on the first clip (0.776 against 0.991) and, on the held-out
one, **inverted**: the reuses that damage the picture are predicted as *cheap*, because the frames that break are
calm and low-texture with small coherent motion - exactly the case a texture-complexity term calls easy.

Caveats on the approximation: one reference instead of three, DIS flow instead of codec motion vectors, `TC` from
an 8-bit luma round trip rather than the codec's own residual. Any of those could matter, but none of them is
needed to explain the sign: a predictor built on texture complexity is, on this material, pointed the wrong way.

## Splitting the correction by frequency, not by gain: both instruments satisfied on 1040 s

The gain scan said one scalar cannot satisfy both instruments. It cannot - but the two components of
`LR_t - LR_ref` are not distinguished by their amplitude, they are distinguished by their **content**: the
displacement/tone part is low-frequency and must be added once, the grain part is high-frequency and the network
amplifies it by `alpha`. So split the residual with a 5 px box low-pass and give the two halves different gains,
applying the grain gain only where it cannot move an edge (local `|r| < 3` levels and LR gradient `< 5`), with the
correction computed **per channel**:

`out = cached_SR + up(lowpass(r) + alpha * highpass(r))`

| clip | variant | frames over 0.1% | texture |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1040 s | verbatim skip | 37 / 130 | 0.000 |
| 1040 s | structural only (alpha 1.0) | 0 / 130 | 0.775 |
| 1040 s | **split, alpha 1.35** | **0 / 130** (worst 0.09166%) | **1.001** |
| 960 s, held out | structural only | 4 / 124 | 0.760 |
| 960 s, held out | split, alpha 1.35 | **4 / 124** (worst 0.19207%) | **1.020** |

Two results. First, **the grain half is free at the acceptance bar**: alpha 1.35 lifts the texture from 0.775 to
1.001 on 1040 s and from 0.760 to 1.020 on the held-out clip without adding a single frame above the bar. Second,
**the four remaining failures on 960 s are geometry, not grain**: the structural-only variant reads the same
4 / 124 and the same worst share, which is the coherent-motion case a non-warped residual cannot fix.

Correction to earlier rows: RU017/RU018 (0.09329% / 0.388) were computed with the LR read as **luma** and the
correction broadcast across the three channels. The per-channel variant reads 0.08046% / 0.775 at the same unit
gain, so the 0.388 was an instrument artefact and the texture deficit was half of what the record said before the
grain half is added.

### Codec motion vectors: extracted, and not usable as read

The queue row for the geometry axis (`rve-reuse-codec-mv-2026-11-20`) reached the first acceptance gate and stopped
there. Extraction works: PyAV surfaces motion vectors only when
`stream.codec_context.options = {"flags2": "+export_mvs"}` is set before decoding - without it the frames carry
timecode only, and `ffprobe` prints the side-data type but not its contents. The field is 16x16 blocks with
destination and source positions and quarter-pel vectors (`motion_scale` 4), and the stream is I B B P, so every
frame carries two reference indices (-1 and +1).

**Verification failed, and that is the finding.** Warping `LR_{t-1}` by the `source == -1` vectors makes the mean
residual over the coded-motion blocks **15.6% worse at the median**, improving only 24% of the 192 hops that carry
a field; on P frames in display order the gain stays inside -9..+9%. The warp is not what fails - the reference is:
`source` is a reference-list index, and the side data does not say which frame it points at. With
`has_b_frames=4` a P frame's list0 entry can be several frames back, so the vector is applied to the wrong picture,
which reads exactly like "the motion vectors are wrong".

So the row is **blocked on a decision**, not on a measurement: a small libavcodec extractor that also reports each
vector's reference frame, a decoder that exposes motion fields with their reference identity, or dropping the
geometry axis and shipping the measured state (split/recursive correction, 0/130 on the calibration clip, 3/124 on
the held-out one, texture ~1.0 on both).

@claim RU035 -15.6 % "frame hops [27bf4d3d46052a40] n=192" measured

## The artifact the acceptance statistic cannot see: frozen grain

The 0.1% share judges a frame against the ungated render; a reused frame carries the *reference's* noise, so
on flat static areas the output stops changing. `scripts/reuse_texture.py` measures exactly that: per frame,
the median over flat static blocks of the block temporal |diff| in the gated render divided by the same in the
ungated one. Ratio ~1 means the output changes like a fresh render; ~0 means the grain is frozen.

On the same 1040 s pair (204 pairs, 130 reused frames, 74 rendered):

| population | n | median ratio | share below 0.5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| reused frames | 130 | **0.000** | **91.5%** |
| reused, **and under** the 0.1% bar | 93 | 0.000 | 88.2% |
| rendered (fresh) | 74 | **1.026** | **0.0%** |

So the acceptance statistic flags 37 frames, while **119 of the 130 reuses are frozen in the flat areas** -
the bar sees less than a third of the artifact class, and 82 of the frames it passes are frozen. That is the

### What the gate buys end-to-end, with the product encoder

Measured on the 960 s cut, three interleaved rounds in one session, the app as it ships (TensorRT, 4K output through
`hevc_nvenc -cq 18`), gate ON against gate OFF:

| configuration | ms/frame | fps |
|---|---|---|
| gate OFF (every frame rendered) | 51.45 | 19.4 |
| gate ON, 124 of 201 frames reused (61.7%) | **26.01** | 38.4 |
| gate ON + the 2.428 ms correction on the reused frames | 27.5 | 36.4 |

So **1.98x** on the render loop, 1.87x once the correction is charged. The same pair through an `ffv1` lossless 4K
sink reads only 1.20x (58.46 -> 48.84 ms/frame) because the lossless encode dominates the fixed cost - the earlier
`4.31x` headline was the engine half times a gate half measured on a different pipeline state, and this is what the
gate half is on the current one.
