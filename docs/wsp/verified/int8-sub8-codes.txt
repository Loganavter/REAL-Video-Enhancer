# Six-bit codes between the hops are dead on the picture: 2.7% of pixels against a 0.1% bar

Emulated 2026-11-20 (local), BD Violet, four held-out frames (29, 44, 274, 400), calibrated on the eight
spread frames. No kernel, no GPU job: the whole falsifier is the corpus's own emulation recipe.
Records: `int8-sub8-codes.jsonl`; raw: `logs/sub8/probe.json`.

Row `rve-sub8-codes-2026-11-19` (researcher's C12). The hop moves 265 MB per launch and sits at ~52% of both
roofs, so traffic is what stops it, and packing the codes between hops to 6 bits would cut that by 25%. The
row was filed low on purpose, because the whole question is the picture - and the emulation answers it without
a kernel.

## Two ways to spend the two bits

| variant | what it keeps | worst share vs 8-bit | peak |
|---|---|---|---|
| rescale, `s' = s*127/31` | the RANGE, 4.1x the step | **2.71%** | 77 levels |
| clip, `clamp(+-31)` | the STEP, a quarter of the range | **3.04%** | 90 levels |

Against the 0.1% bar that is **27-30x over**, on every held-out frame, with peaks near a third of the full
scale. The two variants are close to each other, which says the mechanism is not clipping specifically: two
bits simply cannot carry the body's activations through sixteen dependent requantisations.

## The control that makes the number mean something

The same chain at 8 bits reads **0.01536%** worst frame against the MODEL - the corpus's recorded
body-vs-model range (0.006-0.016%) - so this is the body's arithmetic and not an emulation artefact. The
6-bit readings at 2.7% are 176x that, and 27x over the bar.

## What this closes

The row's own acceptance: *"If the share or the peak leaves its bar, close it."* Both leave it, for both
variants, on all four frames. **No kernel is worth writing, and the traffic roof of the hop is not attackable
this way.** That matters for the one row still open on the hop (`rve-hop-load-schedule`): its ceiling is
bounded by 265 MB of traffic, and this is the measurement that rules out shrinking the codes as the answer -
so the hop's remaining headroom is in the schedule only, down to the ~0.92 ms/hop traffic floor.

## Not covered

- Picture only. Packing and unpacking cost real ALU work (a 6-bit unpack in the load path of a kernel that is
  already at 52% of its compute roof), and nothing here prices it - but the picture closes the question
  before that matters.
- One title, four frames, one model. Per-tensor grids per hop, symmetric, exactly as the shipped body.
- A different code layout (blocked scales, per-group zero points, 6 bits with a shared exponent) is not
  measured; what is measured is the straightforward "6 bits with per-channel scales" the row proposed.
- The control is the emulation's 8-bit chain, not the shipped Triton kernel; their agreement is the corpus's
  existing result (`int8_kernel_emulation.py`), not re-established here.
