# Frame 441 is a reuse, the engine is fine, and the invariant is one-sided

## The worst frame is composition, not the engine

Frame 441 - the int8 worst on Chainsaw - is a **depth-1 reuse**, with an fp16 share of 0.07489% against
int8's 0.08876%. Splitting the whole sweep by population:

| population | frames | int8 max |
|---|---|---|
| reused | 83 | **0.08876%** |
| rendered | 398 | **0.00034%** |

So the engine's own error is 260x below the composition term, and the probe's 0.001% engine reading is
**correct**. The 89x gap is entirely in composition, where the spread sample found no depth-2 pair at
all. "The probe reads the engine 89x light" would have been the wrong diagnosis; the pair selection is
the whole of it.

## The scan costs 11.5 s, not 3

One streaming LR pass, no model: **11.46 s** on Chainsaw, 11.29 on Violet, plus ~6.5 s for the top-13
renders. Against a 45 s probe that is **+40%**, not a rounding error - so enabling it by default is a
real cost decision, not free.

## The invariant is one-sided

On the int8 side the check fires on Violet (d1 0.139 against 0.12727 = 1.09x, d2 0.216 = 1.70x) and
passes on Chainsaw (0.001 against 0.08876) - which is exactly the **under**-read that produces the false
accept. `control <= sweep` catches over-reads only; the dangerous direction is invisible to it, and only
the adversarial path closes it.
