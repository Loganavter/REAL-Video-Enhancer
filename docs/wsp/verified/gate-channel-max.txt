# The defect is in the projection: channel-max separates perfectly where luma cannot

Measured 2026-11-17 by recomputing the same LR statistics in channel-max instead of luma, over the same
100 reused frames of Chainsaw Man 20 s, joined with the per-frame HR share of the gated-vs-ungated
decode.

| feature | AUC |
|---|---|
| share above 8 levels, **any channel** | **1.000** |
| share above 6 levels, any channel | **1.000** |
| share above 4 levels, any channel | 0.999 |
| share above 4 levels, luma (the gate's projection) | 0.993 |
| max channel delta | 0.936 |
| max luma delta | 0.910 |

Frame 164 - the worst frame by HR share and the one no luma statistic could see - is **rank 1 of 100**
by the channel-max share above 8 (0.619%), against rank 27 by the luma share above 4. The class does not
need a second mechanism; the projection does.

## Why luma is blind here

Frame 164 is a clipped highlight: ungated (244, 255, 255), gated (255, 255, 255), so the difference is
**11 levels in R only** and zero in G and B. The gate's luma taps are `(1, 2, 1) / 4`, so R enters with
a weight of 0.25 and the same 11 levels read 2.75 in the gate's variable against 11 in the acceptance
variable - a factor of 4. On a blue highlight the factor would be 4 as well; on BT.709 weights it would
be 4.7. Any luma gate is blind to colour restored on a clipped highlight by construction, and Chainsaw
Man is fire, blood and muzzle flashes.

The change is **real in the LR input**: the mean R delta between frame 164 and its reference 161 over
the flagged area is 11.2 levels (max 15). It is not manufactured by the upscaler - that reading came
from the luma number, where the same change is 2.75.

## What a channel-max gate would cost

The separation at the acceptance threshold itself: bad frames bottom out at 0.0818% and good frames top
out at 0.0651%, a 1.3x gap. Blocking everything above 0.08% blocks **18 of 100 - all 18 bad, zero
good**. Reuse falls to 17.0% and the wall becomes 7.14 + 0.830 x 51.06 = 49.50 ms against 47.58, i.e.
**+4.0%** - and the worst frame is gone, which the luma threshold at 3.0 could not achieve for +2.7%.

The 1.3x gap is thin and the threshold was read off this clip, so it must be calibrated on a population
that contains the failure mode, not on a calm cut.

## The gate's threshold cannot substitute for it

Measured across the whole tuner clamp, not just its upper half: the probe's depth-2 number is 0.2177%
at gate thresholds **5.0, 4.0, 3.0 and 2.0**. The damaged pair is under 2.0 - the tightest setting the
tuner can choose - so no threshold on the luma variable removes it.

## Why the simulator and the app disagree at the boundary

The app converts YUV to RGB with `cv2.cvtColor(yuv, COLOR_YUV2RGB_I420)` - BT.601, full range - while
this simulator decodes with ffmpeg, BT.709, tv range. The luma taps are the same `(1,2,1)/4` in both,
the RGB underneath is not. So the simulator reproduces the app's *decisions* (100 reused, 2 scene cuts)
at threshold 4.0, where the sign of `delta - 4` is not close, and it diverges at the boundary (84
predicted against 88 measured at 3.0). Every absolute threshold quoted from the simulation is on a
slightly different variable than the product's, which is why the channel-max threshold above must be
re-derived on the app's own numbers before it ships.
