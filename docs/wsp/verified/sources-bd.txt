# The input, read instead of trusted: BD cuts, and what the 4 Mbps files were

Written 2026-11-18 after the owner looked at the frames. The lane's prose called
`/tmp/opencode/{violet,csm}20s.mkv` "the BD remaster"; they are **4.038 and 3.982 Mbps** h264
re-encodes of a **37.8 / 37.2 Mbps** BDRemux - 9.4x - and every content number measured from
2026-11-15 to 2026-11-18 inherited the label. This file is the input's own record, and the check that
makes the label mechanical: `PYTHONPATH=. python3 -m harness.cli source-check docs/wsp/sources.yaml`.

## What the files are

| file | bit rate | packets | matrix / range | B-frames | what it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `bd_violet20s.mkv` | **37.715 Mbps** | 495 | undeclared / undeclared | 4 | `-c copy` from Violet Evergarden ep.01 BDRemux |
| `bd_csm20s.mkv` | **39.552 Mbps** | 503 | undeclared / undeclared | 4 | `-c copy` from Chainsaw Man ep.01 BDRemux |
| `violet20s.mkv` | **4.038 Mbps** | 480 | undeclared / tv | 2 | re-encode, labelled "BD" until 2026-11-18 |
| `csm20s.mkv` | **3.982 Mbps** | 480 | undeclared / tv | 2 | re-encode, labelled "BD" until 2026-11-18 |

The sources: Violet Evergarden ep.01 `37.824 Mbps`, 1421.056 s; Chainsaw Man ep.01 `37.215 Mbps`,
1527.030 s - both h264 High, 1920x1080, yuv420p, 24000/1001, and both **declare no colour matrix and
no transfer** (the re-encodes declare `range=tv`; the BD cuts declare nothing). So the replacement does
not fix the colour-space question either: it moves from "undeclared matrix with tv range" to
"undeclared matrix". The app converts with `cv2.COLOR_YUV2RGB_I420` (BT.601 full range) while this
project's ffmpeg-based simulators assume BT.709/tv, and that disagreement is **common-mode** inside the
gated-vs-ungated delta these numbers are - it is not common-mode against the source.

## The cuts are stream copies, proved by packets, not by the bit rate

A `-c copy` cut is an exact slice of the source's video packets; a re-encode is not. The clip's packet
sizes were located as a contiguous run inside the source's:

| clip | source packet offset (first video packet) | packets matched end to end |
|---|---|---|
| `bd_violet20s.mkv` | 14375 | 494 / 495 |
| `bd_csm20s.mkv` | 28273 | 502 / 503 |
| `csm_battle.mkv` | 28273 | 502 / 503 - **the same media as `bd_csm20s.mkv`** |
| `violet_action.mkv` | 20689 | 486 / 487 |
| `csm8s.mkv` | 7169 | 248 / 249 |

Two consequences, both narrowing the damage rather than widening it:

1. **`csm_battle.mkv` is the BD battle cut.** Every number recorded on it already used BD material;
   the noise floor 22.58 and the 0.0% full sweep over 503 frames in `gate-action-scenes.txt` are
   reproduced exactly by this session's `bd_csm20s.mkv` run (22.58, reuse refused). It is a control
   the new pipeline passes to the digit.
2. **`violet_action.mkv` and `csm8s.mkv` are also stream copies**, so the action-scene and 8-second
   rows were not measured on the re-encodes.

## The population changed in two ways at once, and only one of them was the codec

`bd_violet20s.mkv` covers the **same shot** as `violet20s.mkv`: frame `a[i]` of the re-encode matches
frame `b[i+9..11]` of the BD cut at a mean absolute difference of 0.23-0.25 levels over the whole
clip (a static opening makes the lag the only reliable handle; beyond frame 180 the same-index MAD
rises to 2.6-3.3, which is the 9-11 frame offset). The BD cut starts ~9 frames earlier because
`-c copy` can only cut on a keyframe.

`bd_csm20s.mkv` does **not** cover the same shot as `csm20s.mkv`: no frame of the re-encode matches
any frame of that cut better than 13.7 levels MAD, and the best matches scatter (331, 219, 477, 414).
The CSM replacement clip is a different scene. Old-vs-new CSM comparisons are therefore
**cross-population on scene as well as codec**, and nothing here isolates the codec for CSM except the
`csm_battle` control above.

The frame counts differ too (495/503 against 480/480), so a frame index from one cut does not name the
same picture in the other. That is written into every population label below.

## What the check does, and what it cannot do

`python -m harness.cli source-check docs/wsp/sources.yaml` re-reads bit rate, packet count, pixel
format, colour tags and B-frame depth, refuses a BD label below 35 Mbps, and - with `--prove` -
re-runs the packet-subsequence proof. `--records` lists every record whose population items name a
contaminated input: **124 records in 24 verified files**, and they include the two load-bearing
content verdicts.

It also exposes a second, quieter defect: **20 records in 3 files store a population of bare
`frame-N` with no file name at all** (`int8-live`, `gate-default-vs-ungated`, `int8-window`). Their
item-set hash cannot distinguish two different clips at the same frame index, so contamination does not
propagate to them mechanically. `gate-default-vs-ungated.txt` is the evidence under
`gate-exceeds-acceptance-bar`, and it was measured on `csm20s.mkv` - the file is named in the prose and
absent from the record.

What is NOT checked: whether the population is the one a decision needs; whether the app's assumed
colour matrix equals the file's; whether a frame index from another cut names the same picture.
