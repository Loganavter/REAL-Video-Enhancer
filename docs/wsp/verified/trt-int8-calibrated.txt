# TensorRT's own int8, calibrated: 1.21x over its own fp16, and 1% of a dark frame off by more than 8 codes

Measured 2026-11-20 on the 4060 Ti, TensorRT **10.12.0.36** (the app's own version), `trtexec 10.12.0.36`
installed without root. Model `2x-Futsuu-Anime(1).pth` -> ONNX opset 17, static 1x3x1080x1920, builder level 5.
Records `trt-int8-calibrated.jsonl` (TI001-TI026), raw `logs/trt-int8/` (three profiles, the accuracy JSON, the
build manifest, the build log).

Why this page: the lane had closed the **explicit Q/DQ** route (`engine-int8.txt`: lossless-but-slower, 64-70 ms
against 50-56 fp16, because a per-channel PReLU cannot join an int8 epilogue). It had never built TensorRT's own
**implicit** int8 with a calibrator - the vendor's quantiser on the same graph, which is the one comparison that
can say whether the hand-written Triton body was needed.

## What was built

Three engines from one ONNX, same builder settings, only the quantisation arm differing:

| arm | calibrator | build | size |
|---|---|---|---|
| fp16 | - | 193.496 s | 3.5 MiB |
| int8 entropy | `IInt8EntropyCalibrator2` over 8 named frames | 170.854 s | 1.3 MiB |
| int8 min-max | `IInt8MinMaxCalibrator` over the same 8 frames | 167.876 s | 1.26 MiB |

The calibration population is eight frames, four from each bluray cut the lane renders (`bd_violet20s`,
`bd_csm_same`), sha256 in `trt-int8-manifest.json`; the evaluation frames are **different timestamps from the same
cuts** (`eval_violet` at 5 s, `eval_csm` at 10 s), because a delta measured on the calibration set is a fit, not a
result. The calibration cache is 2.5 KB and is written next to the engine, so a rebuild needs no frames.

## Speed: 1.21x, and the graph says why it is not more

| engine | layers | ms | vs its own fp16 |
|---|---|---|---|
| fp16 | **25** | **46.854** | - |
| int8 entropy | **60** | **38.867** | **1.205x** |
| int8 min-max | **60** | **38.875** | 1.205x |

Both calibrators land within 0.008 ms of each other: the algorithm is not the variable. The layer count is: int8
takes the 25-layer fused graph and splits it back to 60, because **the per-channel PReLU cannot join an int8
epilogue** - the same mechanism `engine-int8.txt` found on the Q/DQ build, and it is structural, not a
calibrator artefact. Every hop pays a quantise/dequantise boundary again.

So TensorRT's int8 buys **1.21x** over TensorRT's fp16 on this architecture. The app's int8 body buys **1.665x**
end-to-end (JC013) and its body alone reads 26.1 ms where the fp16 engine's body-equivalent layers are 46.4 ms.

## Picture: one held-out frame is fine, the other is not

Against the fp16 engine, channel-max |delta| in 8-bit codes:

| frame | arm | max | mean | share >8 codes | PSNR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| csm t=10 | entropy | 5.85 | 0.53 | **0.0000%** | 51.57 dB |
| csm t=10 | min-max | 10.09 | 1.09 | 0.0003% | 45.24 dB |
| violet t=5 | entropy | **91.14** | 0.80 | **1.0715%** | 39.34 dB |
| violet t=5 | min-max | 20.86 | 1.12 | 0.0136% | 44.95 dB |

The lane's own bar for the int8 body is **0.1% of pixels above 8 levels**, and the shipped body measures
**0.00125%** on 494 frames (K=2; 0.00353% per-tensor). On one held-out frame the entropy-calibrated TRT int8 is at
**1.07%** - ten times the bar, and ~850 times the body's rate - with single pixels off by 91 codes. Min-max trades
the peak for a uniform bias: its mean error doubles and 43-45% of samples sit off by more than one code.

## Where the error is: the dark end, everywhere

Diagnostic on the failing frame (entropy arm, worst six pixels): the reference is dark - 0.06-0.17 in 0-1 units -
and the int8 engine returns 0.37-0.51. It **lifts the shadows by about 0.3**, i.e. 80 codes, and does it across the
whole frame, not in a highlight: 1.32% of pixels have some channel off by more than 8 codes, and the row and column
profiles are flat (1.2-2.4% per decile band). The frame is not unusual either - max luma 225, mean 94 - while the
*clean* frame is the bright one (2.26% of pixels above 250).

That is consistent with what the hardware allows: **symmetric-only quantisation** on this GPU (a non-zero
zero-point is a DLA-only switch, `engine-int8.txt`), plus 16 PReLU boundaries each rounding the same activation.
The shadows are where a symmetric range spends the fewest codes, and sixteen boundaries accumulate it.

## What it means for the lane

- **The hand body is justified by measurement, not by preference.** On the same model and card the vendor's
  int8 gives 1.21x over its own fp16 and a visible shadow lift; the body gives 1.665x end-to-end and 0.00125% above
  the bar over 494 frames.
- **The two speeds are not one ratio.** The engine numbers here are a whole-frame `trtexec` build; the body's
  26.1 ms is the app's phase counter on its tiled engine. What is same-instrument is: 46.854 vs 38.867 (trtexec),
  and 52.39 vs 31.47 (the app). The conclusion does not depend on mixing them.
- **What TRT int8 would need to compete** is per-layer precision control (keep head and tail fp16 - the Q/DQ page
  did exactly that and still lost), asymmetric activation quantisation (not available), and the 16 PReLU
  boundaries removed (a kernel, i.e. the body). None of that is cheaper than the body that exists.
- **Not pursued, and why it is written down:** a 494-frame pair of the calibrated engine would cost two renders to
  confirm what one held-out frame already shows by two orders of magnitude.

## Reproduce

```bash
python3 scripts/trt_int8_calibrate.py --onnx /tmp/opencode/futsuu.onnx --frames /tmp/opencode/calib-frames \
    --out /tmp/opencode --opt 5            # fp16, int8-entropy, int8-minmax + the manifest
~/.local/bin/trtexec --loadEngine=/tmp/opencode/futsuu_int8_entropy.trt --dumpProfile --iterations=20 --avgRuns=20
python3 scripts/trt_int8_accuracy.py --fp16 /tmp/opencode/futsuu_fp16.trt \
    --int8 /tmp/opencode/futsuu_int8_entropy.trt --int8 /tmp/opencode/futsuu_int8_minmax.trt \
    --frames /tmp/opencode/eval-frames --height 1080 --width 1920
PYTHONPATH=scripts python3 scripts/trt_int8_records.py
PYTHONPATH=scripts python3 -m harness.cli check docs/wsp/verified/trt-int8-calibrated.txt \
    --records docs/wsp/verified/records/trt-int8-calibrated.jsonl --bars docs/claims-bars.json
```
