# The hop has no per-launch constant: it scales with work, and the "constant" was the first tap's latency

Measured 2026-11-19 (local), the hop kernel at five frame sizes, three draws of twenty launches each, medians.
Records: `int8-hop-constant.jsonl`; raw: `logs/hop-constant/probe.json`.

Why this exists: an outside reader's first recommendation was "9.44 ms/frame sit in a 0.59 ms per-launch
constant - trace it with nsys, and if it is host dispatch, CUDA graphs kill it for free". That is only true if the
cost is per *launch*. The cheap decisive test is to shrink the frame: a per-launch cost stays exactly the same at
256x256, a per-work cost falls with the pixels.

## The sweep

| frame | px | ms | ms/Mpx | TOPS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 256x256 | 0.07 M | **0.0572** | 0.873 | 84.4 |
| 512x512 | 0.26 M | 0.207 | 0.788 | 93.6 |
| 1024x1024 | 1.05 M | 0.811 | 0.773 | 95.3 |
| 1920x1080 | 2.07 M | **1.595** | 0.769 | 95.9 |
| 3840x2160 | 8.29 M | **5.912** | **0.713** | 103.4 |

**127x the pixels costs 103x the time, and the per-pixel cost IMPROVES with size** (0.873 -> 0.713 ms/Mpx). The
implied per-launch term is **0.011 ms** - eighteen times smaller than a millisecond, i.e. nothing.

## What this refutes

1. **"The hop pays a 0.59 ms launch constant, so 9.44 ms/frame sits there."** No. There is no launch constant
   worth naming. What the earlier decomposition measured is a **sub-linear tap cost**: removing taps makes the
   first one cost 0.59 ms and the next eight 0.06 ms each. That is memory-level parallelism inside one launch -
   with one tap in flight the kernel waits on it, with nine it overlaps them - not a fixed fee per launch.
2. **"CUDA graphs would recover it."** There is nothing to recover: the launch term is 0.011 ms. Graphs may still
   be worth it for the *17 launches* of the frame (0.011 x 17 = 0.19 ms), which is 0.6% of the loop and not the
   headline the estimate suggested.
3. **"The cascade saves 0.59 ms per launch removed."** It does not. Cascade still has a real justification, but a
   different one: a fused pair never writes and re-reads the intermediate, so each fusion removes ~250 MB of
   DRAM per hop-pair - the traffic argument, which was already in the row, not the launch argument.
4. **Batch M x 2 was flat** now reads as expected rather than as evidence against host overhead: batching halves
   the *number of launches per frame* and there was almost nothing there to halve.

## What it leaves standing

- The kernel is **throughput-bound and works fine**: 96-103 TOPS at every size, and the per-pixel cost falls
  slightly at 4K because the launch tail is amortised better - the opposite of a fixed fee.
- The 0.59 ms is therefore **work**: the first tap's loads at the start of every launch, before the pipeline is
  full. Removing it means either filling that pipeline sooner (shared memory, measured negative) or removing
  taps (Winograd, measured at the ridge and closed).
- The honest statement for the ledger: **the hop's cost is 1.55 ms of work at 1080p, 57% of both roofs, with a
  sub-linear tap profile whose first tap is expensive because nothing else is in flight yet.** No constant, no
  graphs.

## Not covered

- One card, one kernel, synthetic code buffers (the shape is what was varied; values do not change timing).
- 3840x2160 reads 103.4 TOPS against 96 at 1080p, which is the launch tail amortising, not a different kernel.
