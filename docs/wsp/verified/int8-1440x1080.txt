# The body works at LR 1440x1080: 0.012% against the model, and the frame is 1.22x the fp16 engine

Measured 2026-11-19 on a 1440x1080 version of the BD Violet cut. Why: the body's accuracy had only ever been
recorded at LR 1920x1080, while the film lane runs at 1440x1080 (94 frames, 1440p -> 2880p, `docs/MODEL.md`),
and the app's own envelope allows anything up to 2.5 Mpx of LR input. "It will work at 1440x1080" was a size
comparison, not a measurement.

## The input, and why its label matters

`/tmp/opencode/violet1440.mkv`: 494 frames, 20.6 s, **1440x1080 [SAR 4:3 DAR 16:9]**, yuv420p progressive,
**18.4 Mbps h264 CRF-12**, produced by `scale=1440:1080` on the packet-proved BD cut (`bd_violet20s.mkv`)
followed by a re-encode. Three consequences of that provenance, all of them narrowing:

- it is **not a stream copy**: the picture went through a scaler and an encoder, so its grain is not the BD's;
- it is **anamorphic** (SAR 4:3, DAR 16:9): the 1920-wide frame was squeezed into 1440 storage, so horizontal
  detail is compressed and the body sees a horizontally-reduced picture. That geometry is what a 1080i
  anamorphic film source stores, so the *shape* is representative of the film lane even though the *material*
  is not;
- it is **not the film clip** MODEL.md names (94 frames, 1440p -> 2880p): nothing here describes that clip's
  grain, telecine or compression artefacts.

So: the answer to "does the body work at 1440x1080" is measured, on a squeezed re-encode of anime; the film
lane's own material remains unmeasured.

**A native 1440x1080 measurement now exists** (2026-11-19, corrected: an earlier version of this paragraph
claimed no such source was on this machine, which was wrong - the search looked at `*.mkv` only and missed
`remastering/Untitled78.mov`). The cut: `/tmp/opencode/native1440.mkv`, stream copy at 1:00, 496 frames,
**HEVC Main 10, yuv420p10le, 1440x1080 [SAR 1:1 DAR 4:3]**, 23.98 fps, 10 Mbps. Owner's note on it: **no
grain** - it is clean material, so this measures the resolution question, not grain.

| calibration | cost | worst share | peak |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24 full frames (spread over the clip) | 5.29 s | **0.03601%** | **16** |
| 96 crops of 256x256 | 0.46 s | **0.02826%** | 17 |

Both are ~3x under the 0.1% share bar and, unlike the synthetic clip, the peak (16-17) is **under**
`bar_int8_peak` = 20 as well. Everything else about the envelope is unchanged: LR 1.56 Mpx, 64 body channels,
whole-frame path.

## Accuracy against the model (the reference the record requires)

`harness/scripts/calibration_coverage.py --width 1440 --height 1080`, 24 calibration frames, four held-out test
frames (29, 44, 274, 400 - none in either calibration arm), body against the model's own fp16 output:

| calibration | cost | worst share | peak |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24 full frames | 5.24 s | **0.01186%** | 22 |
| 96 crops of 256x256 | 0.47 s | **0.01347%** | 22 |

Both are ~8x under the 0.1% share bar. The peak (22) sits just above `bar_int8_peak` = 20 - consistent with
the known `int8-peak-unmet` debt (29-55 recorded on the earlier kernel family), not a new failure.

## The frame in the app

One run per arm, null sink, reuse off, same clip:

| arm | render loop | body | hops | head | tail |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fp16 engine | 43.71 ms/frame | - | - | - | - |
| int8 body | **35.80 ms/frame** | 32.6 | 22.6 | 3.9 + 1.4 quantise | 4.7 |

**1.22x on the frame**, in one run each - not a bar claim (that needs >=3 rounds, medians of 2+), but it
bounds the film lane: the body is allowed here (1.56 Mpx against the 2.5 Mpx refusal) and it pays.

The tail at this size: dequant 1.17, conv 1.97, shuffle 0.90, residual 0.64 = 4.68 ms, i.e. the tail conv
scales with the LR area as expected (1.97 against 2.35 at 1080p) because it runs on LR.

## What this closes and what it does not

- **Closes**: the body is measured at the film lane's resolution, accuracy first, with the app's envelope
  respected. The answer to "will 1440x1080 work" is yes, with the numbers above.
- **Does not close**: the peak bar on the *synthetic* clip (22 > 20) - on native clean material it is met
  (16-17); grain-heavy native 1440p is still unmeasured, and the owner states this source has no grain; the
  1.22x on the synthetic clip is one draw per arm.

## Correction to this file's own provenance claim

The first version of this paragraph said a native 1440x1080 source "is not on this machine", based on a
search for recent `*.mkv` files only. `remastering/Untitled78.mov` is a native 1440x1080 HEVC 10-bit source
and the owner pointed it out. The claim was wrong and is corrected here rather than deleted, because the way
it was wrong - a filename filter mistaken for an inventory - is the same failure class as a population label
mistaken for a population.

## Reproduce

```bash
<ffmpeg> -i /tmp/opencode/bd_violet20s.mkv -vf scale=1440:1080 -c:v libx264 -crf 12 -an /tmp/opencode/violet1440.mkv
flock -n /var/tmp/opencode/gpu.lock env PYTHONPATH=. <install-python> harness/scripts/calibration_coverage.py \
    --clip /tmp/opencode/violet1440.mkv --width 1440 --height 1080 --calib 24 \
    --model "<install>/custom_models/2x-Futsuu-Anime(1).pth"
CLIPS=violet1440.mkv ROUNDS=1 SINK=null LOGDIR=<dir> scripts/wall_ab.sh
```

Run: PYTHONPATH=. python3 -m harness.cli check docs/wsp/verified/int8-1440x1080.txt \
