# The tail writes yuv420p itself: -2.16 ms in the app, 0.00000% on 494 frames

Measured 2026-11-19/20 (local). Records: `int8-tail-csc.jsonl` (isolated N001-N003, in-app JC001-JC010,
pair TC001-TC004); raw: `logs/tail-csc/probe.json`, `logs/tail-csc-ratio/`, `logs/tail-csc-pair/`; kernel:
`tail_int8_yuv_kernel` in `backend/src/pytorch/Int8Kernels.py`, knob `RVE_TAIL_YUV` in `Int8Body`.

Row `rve-tail-csc-yuv-2026-11-20`, from an outside reader who noticed two things the lane had not: the pack's
4.37 ms is **not a copy** (the stream pipeline measured -0.01) but YCbCr arithmetic over 8.3 Mpx; and
**PixelShuffle(2) makes 4:2:0 chroma register-local** - the 12 tail channels of one input pixel become a 2x2
output block, and that block is exactly what the chroma average covers.

## Why it can be done inside the tail kernel at all

Y, Cb and Cr are each LINEAR in (R, G, B), so each is a weighted sum over the channel axis of the reshaped
(16, ...) epilogue tile. With BT.709 and limited range:

```
Y  =  0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B                    -> Yq  = round(219 Y + 16),   clamp 16..235
Cb = (B - Y) * 0.5/(1-0.0722) = -0.11459 R - 0.38547 G + 0.50000 B
Cr = (R - Y) * 0.5/(1-0.2126) =  0.50000 R - 0.45419 G - 0.04585 B
Uq = round(224 mean(Cb) + 128)                        -> clamp 16..240
```

The residual adds the SAME LR pixel to all four subpixels, so its contribution collapses to one value per
position per output channel - no second pass. Output: **6 bytes per input pixel** (4 Y in the 2H x 2W plane, then
U and V in two H x W planes) = 12.44 MB against the 49.77 MB of fp16 RGB the tail writes otherwise.

## The payload agrees, and that was checked before the kernel existed

An offline gate ran both orders in torch from the SAME tail output: **max |delta| 1 level** over the
12 441 600-byte payload (4.6% of bytes off by one, 0.00000% off by more). So this is a **different-arithmetic**
change with a bounded one-level difference - the rounding moves relative to the chroma average - and the
acceptance criterion is the render pair, not byte-identity.

## Timing, isolated, with the conversion inside each arm

| arm | ms |
|---|---|
| tail kernel -> fp16 RGB (49.77 MB) -> `tensor_to_yuv420p` | **5.123** |
| tail kernel -> yuv420p (12.44 MB) directly | **0.869** |

## In the app: -2.16 ms, and the whole of it lands in the pack

The isolated 4.25 was never going to be the loop: the pack keeps its 12.44 MB transfer. What the app shows,
three arms interleaved in ONE session, medians of rounds 2-3, null sink, 494 frames per draw:

| arm | ms/frame |
|---|---|
| fp16 engine | **52.39** |
| int8, tail writes fp16 RGB, pack converts (`RVE_TAIL_YUV=0`) | **33.63** |
| int8, tail writes yuv420p (`RVE_TAIL_YUV=1`) | **31.47** |

Ratios from the records (a quotient of two records, not a typed number): **1.665x** against the fp16 engine,
**1.558x** without the change - so this row is worth **1.069x on the int8 arm** (JC004, JC005, JC006).

With `hevc_nvenc` in the loop - the product's own condition - the same two arms read JC011 / JC012 and the
product's ratio is **JC013 = 1.667x**, i.e. the encoder changes nothing here: on this clip it is almost entirely
overlapped and both arms sit within 0.2 ms of their null-sink numbers. The stale product figure this replaces
(1.511x) predates both the halo buffers and the shuffle fold.

The phase split says where it comes from, and it disagrees with the isolated prediction in an instructive way:

| phase | conv (RGB) | yuv | what it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| tail | 1.00 | 1.00 | the fused CSC is NOT cheaper than the shuffle it replaces: 24.9 MB of fp16 stores became 12.44 MB of bytes, but the epilogue now does the colour arithmetic. Isolated this read 0.869 against ~1.0 - in the app it is a wash |
| pack+queue | 4.37 | **2.06** | the whole saving: YCbCr over 8.3 Mpx disappeared; what remains is one DMA (12.44 MB at the 6.55 GB/s measured under load, 1.9 ms) plus the queue |

So the honest attribution is **-2.3 ms of arithmetic out of the pack**, not "the tail got faster". It also means
the pack now sits ~0.16 ms above its floor: what is left is the link, not work.

## The picture: 0.00000% on 494 frames

Different arithmetic, so byte-identity was never the expectation. The instrument is the project's own: the share
of pixels with any channel more than 8 levels off, on the worst frame of the pair, bar 0.1%.

```
494 frames, block 64, reference-kind config (RVE_TAIL_YUV=1 against RVE_TAIL_YUV=0)
  worst share 0.00000% (frame 0), frames above 0.1%: 0
  worst block mean 0.52 levels (frame 278), peak 6 levels
```

**0.00000% of pixels, peak 6 levels.** The six is the peak of a one-level payload difference read back through a
4:4:4 ffv1 sink - inside the one-level rounding the offline gate predicted. The worst block mean (0.52 levels) is
the more sensitive read, because a systematic shift would show there rather than in a peak, and it is half a
level.

## The safety coupling, now load-bearing

Writing the payload directly removes the `cat()` intermediate that made the old payload copy effectively
synchronous (`frame-pipeline.txt` section 2). The copy now goes into a pinned slot under the same event guard, and
**`RVE_PAYLOAD_GUARD=1` is not optional on this path**: the guard is what keeps the writer from reading a slot
whose DMA has not landed, and without it the 12.6 dB race that once disabled the pooled path returns. That is why
the guard became a default rather than a knob that happens to be on.

## Not covered

- One clip (BD Violet), one card. The pair is 494 frames of one title; a title whose chroma character differs is
  untested, and the matrix is the BT.709 the source declares.
- The 0.00000% is a pair of two CONFIGURATIONS, not against the model: it measures this change, not the lane's
  total distance from fp16.
- The ffv1 sink carries 4:4:4, so the pair was decoded after ffmpeg upsampled 4:2:0 to 4:4:4 on both arms. A
  chroma defect both arms share through that upsample would be invisible here - the same limitation the halo and
  ring identity pairs have.
- 8-bit SDR only, and that was RUN, not argued: the 10-bit native clip (`native1440_20s.mkv`, 1440x1080 HEVC
  10-bit) rendered with `RVE_TAIL_YUV=1` keeps the old path end to end - `10-bit source detected`, no `8-bit
  pipe: packing yuv420p` line, no `int8 tail writes the payload` line, and the frame handover still reports
  `out dtype torch.float16 shape (1, 3, 2160, 2880)` (`logs/tail-csc-10bit/`). The gate is
  `tail_yuv and yuv8_output and pipe_pixel_format == "yuv420p"`, i.e. it follows the pipe decision.
  That check also surfaced a PRE-EXISTING divergence in the same corner - the packer derives `yuv8_output`
  from the bit depth alone while the writer derives the pipe from the source's declared matrix, so an 8-bit
  source declaring bt601 gets yuv420p bytes on an rgb24 pipe. It is not caused by this row and is filed as
  `rve-yuv8-flag-divergence-2026-11-20`.

```bash
# isolated: the kernel against the old conversion, with the conversion inside each arm
flock -n /var/tmp/opencode/gpu.lock <install-python> scripts/tail_csc_probe.py --rounds 3 --iters 20 \
    --out-file docs/wsp/verified/logs/tail-csc/probe.json
PYTHONPATH=scripts python3 scripts/tail_csc_records.py

# in the app: three arms, one session, interleaved
ARMS="fp16 conv yuv" ARM_ENV_CONV="RVE_TAIL_YUV=0" ARM_ENV_YUV="RVE_TAIL_YUV=1" \
  CLIPS="bd_violet20s.mkv" ROUNDS=3 SINK=null LOGDIR=docs/wsp/verified/logs/tail-csc-ratio \
  scripts/wall_ab.sh

# the picture: a lossless pair, then the project's own acceptance statistic
ARMS="conv yuv" ARM_ENV_CONV="RVE_TAIL_YUV=0" ARM_ENV_YUV="RVE_TAIL_YUV=1" \
  CLIPS="bd_violet20s.mkv" ROUNDS=1 SINK=ffv1 LOGDIR=docs/wsp/verified/logs/tail-csc-pair \
  RENDERS=/home/jorj/2Tb/tail-csc scripts/wall_ab.sh
PYTHONPATH=scripts python3 scripts/gate_sweep.py <conv.mkv> <yuv.mkv> \
    --out docs/wsp/verified/logs/tail-csc-pair/pair.json --reference-kind config \
    --clip-name bd_violet20s.mkv --id-prefix TC \
    --artifact docs/wsp/verified/logs/tail-csc-pair/pair.json \
    --emit docs/wsp/verified/logs/tail-csc-pair/pair-records.jsonl \
    --run "scripts/gate_sweep.py over two ffv1 renders" --session s-tail-csc-2026-11-19
```
