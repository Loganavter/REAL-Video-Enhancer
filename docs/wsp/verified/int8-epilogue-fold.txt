# The epilogue fold: the sign is stable in every interleaved comparison, the size is not

Measured 2026-11-20 (local), RTX 4060 Ti, int8 body (16 hops, 64 channels, BLOCK_M 128, 4 warps), BD Violet
1920x1080. Two isolated sessions (8 rounds x 20 launches and an earlier 4-round one), an in-app interleaved
A/B of the same configuration with `RVE_INT8_EPILOGUE_FOLD` 0/1 (null sink, 3 rounds), and a lossless render
pair of 494 frames.
Records: `int8-epilogue-fold.jsonl`; raw: `logs/epilogue-fold/probe-r8.json`, `logs/epilogue-fold/ab/`.

Row `rve-epilogue-divide-2026-11-19` (researcher's C5). The premise: the epilogue computes
`v = acc*w_scale + bias`, applies PReLU, then `q = floor(v/out_scale + 0.5)` - an fp32 divide per element
over 132 M elements, in the one kernel all sixteen hops run. PReLU is homogeneous of degree one, so a
positive rescale commutes with it: with `k = w_scale/out_scale`, `c = bias/out_scale` the epilogue becomes
one FMA, the PReLU, one add and a floor. The estimated prize was 1.3-2.7 ms of 26.95.

## The eight-round session: fold is faster in 8 of 8 rounds

| round | hop fold=0 | hop fold=1 | chain fold=0 | chain fold=1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.786 | 1.774 | 29.113 | 28.669 |
| 2 | 1.817 | 1.755 | 29.562 | 29.449 |
| 3 | 1.906 | 1.811 | 29.605 | 28.825 |
| 4 | 1.836 | 1.774 | 29.268 | 28.589 |
| 5 | 1.832 | 1.766 | 29.322 | 28.389 |
| 6 | 1.763 | 1.666 | 29.299 | 28.796 |
| 7 | 1.828 | 1.772 | 29.480 | 29.361 |
| 8 | 1.869 | 1.832 | 29.835 | 28.835 |
| **median r2+** | **1.832** | **1.772** | **29.480** | **28.825** |

**One hop: -0.060 ms (-3.3%), faster in 8 rounds of 8. Sixteen hops: -0.655 ms (-2.2%), also 8 of 8.**
So the row's first question has an answer: the hop is not bound by the divide, but the epilogue's instruction
count does show once the launches are dependent - and the estimate of 1.3-2.7 ms was too large by about 4x.

## But the size does not reproduce, and the app sees less of it

| instrument, session | hops (or chain) | frame |
|---|---|---|
| isolated, 8 rounds (above) | **-0.655** | - |
| isolated, earlier 4-round session | -0.255 | - |
| in-app phase counter, 3 interleaved rounds | **-0.16** | - |
| in-app render loop, same 3 rounds | - | **-0.22** |

An earlier 4-round session on the same kernel (before a dead-store cleanup that leaves both arms' arithmetic
unchanged - the code diff after it is still 0.029%, the same 38 463 codes) read the single hop at **1.805
(fold=0) against 1.806 (fold=1)** - parity - and only 2 of its 4 rounds favoured either arm. So the isolated
one-hop instrument does not resolve an effect of this size across sessions, and the chain swings between
-0.25 and -0.66 ms. What is stable across all of it is the **sign**: every interleaved comparison in every
session favours the fold.

The in-app numbers are the ones that describe a frame, and they are the smallest: **-0.16 ms of hops,
-0.22 ms of the frame**, the same direction in 3 rounds of 3 on both instruments. That is 0.45% of a 48.7 ms
frame.

## The picture: below the statistic's own threshold

Lossless ffv1 renders of both configurations, one session, then `gate_sweep --reference-kind config`:

```
494 frames, block 64
worst share 0.00000% (frame 0), frames above 0.1%: 0
worst block mean 0.73 (frame 188), peak 6
```

`peak 6` is below the 8-level threshold the statistic counts at, so the change is invisible to the project's
decision statistic on this clip. The codes say it more precisely: one hop produces **0 differing codes** of
132.7 M, and after sixteen hops **0.029%** differ, worst 53. The rewrite is exact in real arithmetic, so the
fp32 rounding only ever flips a floor at a boundary - hop 1 is bit-identical, and drift appears only where
it accumulates.

## Decision: the knob stays, off by default

- The effect is real in sign, small in size (0.2-0.7 ms of a 48.7 ms frame), and **not resolved to better
  than a factor of three** across instruments.
- It is a different-arithmetic change: peak 6 between two int8 configurations, 0.029% of codes after sixteen
  hops. Adopting it means the shipped render stops being bit-identical to the recorded one, which costs every
  future byte-identity comparison a re-baseline.
- The check adoption would need has not been taken: **the folded arm against the MODEL**, not against the
  shipped int8 arm. The lane kept the per-tensor grid over a per-channel one on exactly that ground
  (`Int8Body._build`: 1.03% vs 0.91% against the fp16 reference).

So `RVE_INT8_EPILOGUE_FOLD` defaults to 0 and the shipped path is bit-identical to the kernel at HEAD.
`fold=1` is one environment variable away, and the adopt path is written down here rather than implied.

**The knob is neutral when off - verified, not assumed.** `scripts/epilogue_control.py` loads the HEAD source
as a separate module and compares codes hop by hop: `FOLD=0` gives **0 differing codes** on hop 1 and **0 over
all sixteen hops, worst per-hop max 0**. A compile-time branch that is supposed to vanish must be shown to.

## Not covered

- One card, one clip, one frame for the isolated arms; the in-app pair is 494 frames of one title.
- The mechanism is not separated: fewer instructions in the epilogue is the natural reading, but a register
  count change, a different scheduling of the same instructions, and an over-read `w_scale` are not excluded.
- Nothing here measures the fold against the model.
- Timing arms are null sink; the gate is off (`--no_temporal_reuse`).

## Reproduce

```bash
flock -n /var/tmp/opencode/gpu.lock <install-python> scripts/hop_epilogue_probe.py --rounds 8 --iters 20 \
    --out-file docs/wsp/verified/logs/epilogue-fold/probe-r8.json
flock -n /var/tmp/opencode/gpu.lock <install-python> scripts/epilogue_control.py
ARMS="nofold fold" ARM_ENV_NOFOLD="RVE_INT8_EPILOGUE_FOLD=0" ARM_ENV_FOLD="RVE_INT8_EPILOGUE_FOLD=1" \
  CLIPS="bd_violet20s.mkv" ROUNDS=3 SINK=null scripts/wall_ab.sh
PYTHONPATH=scripts python3 scripts/gate_sweep.py <nofold.mkv> <fold.mkv> --out pair.json \
  --emit pair-records.jsonl --reference-kind config --clip-name bd_violet20s.mkv \
  --label "..." --config "..." --instrument "..." --session "..." --run "..." --artifact "..." --id-prefix EF
PYTHONPATH=scripts python3 scripts/epilogue_records.py --pair <pair-records.jsonl>
```
