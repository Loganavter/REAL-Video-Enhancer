# The persistent-CTA grid is 2.2x slower: the hop has no schedule left

Measured 2026-11-20 (local), RTX 4060 Ti (34 SMs), one hop of the int8 body on its own codes (frame 169 of BD
Violet), three arms in one process, 3 interleaved rounds, medians of rounds 2+.
Records: `int8-hop-persistent.jsonl`; raw: `logs/hop-persistent/probe.json`.

Row `rve-hop-persistent-cta-2026-11-20` - the third and last schedule idea, named in `int8-hop-loads.txt` as
the one variant the earlier negatives did not cover. Two were already measured dead: vectorised loads (+0.9%,
the compiler was doing it) and the weight hoist (-54%, two accumulators spill).

| arm | CTAs | ms | TOPS | % of 176 | GB/s | % of 288 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| shipped, one CTA per tile | 16 200 | **1.703** | 89.8 | 51.0% | 155.6 | 54.0% |
| persistent, one CTA per SM | 34 | 3.709 | 41.2 | 23.4% | 71.4 | 24.8% |
| persistent, two per SM | 68 | 2.251 | 67.9 | 38.6% | 117.7 | 40.9% |

**Both persistent grids are slower - 2.2x and 1.3x - and all three arms produce 0 differing codes of 132.7 M**,
so this is one schedule against another and not one arithmetic against another.

## Why it loses, in one sentence

Forty-eight CTAs cannot hide the dependent chain inside a tile: each CTA loops over ~476 tiles *sequentially*,
and at 34 resident CTAs the card has no second wave to overlap the loads of one tile with the arithmetic of
another. The shipped grid's 16 200 CTAs are exactly what a latency-bound kernel needs - the launch overhead
the row hoped to remove (a few hundred µs at 5-27 µs per launch, and this is ONE launch anyway) was never the
cost.

## What this closes, for good

With the traffic lever dead on the picture (`int8-sub8-codes`: 6-bit codes read 2.71% of pixels) and all three
schedule variants measured negative, **25.7 ms of sixteen hops is the floor for this shape on this card**. The
honest statement of what binds it is not "bad scheduling" but "nine gathers and one 64-byte write per pixel,
and the two costs do not overlap" - 51% of the arithmetic roof and 54% of the traffic roof at the same time is
what that shape does here.

That means the frame's remaining headroom is entirely outside the hop: the deliverable pack (3.59 ms, floor
2.44), and the ~10 ms of non-body time the loop still carries.

## Not covered

- One card, one hop, one frame's codes. `tl.make_block_ptr` was not used (a 3x3 window is not a block); the
  load-path hint WAS measured (`int8-hop-loads.txt`) and moved nothing.
- A grid between 68 CTAs and 16 200 was not swept: the trend between 34 and 68 is monotone and the shipped
  grid is 240x the larger persistent one, so a middle point would have to beat both ends of a 1.7x span.
- Nothing here says another card behaves the same - an SM count and a memory system are properties of a card.
