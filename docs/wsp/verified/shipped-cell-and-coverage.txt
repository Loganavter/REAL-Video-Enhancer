## The shipped cell is fp16 + luma, and int8 is refused there

Under the product's defaults the probe reads `composition 0.001%` - but that is **d1**, and the verdict
is taken on d2:

```
composition d1 0.001% / d2 0.220% -> worst 0.220% > bar 0.1% ... -> fp16
```

So int8 + luma is not a configuration the product ships: the probe refuses it on depth 2. The shipped
combination is **fp16 + luma**, whose full sweep is 0.49372% with **18 frames above the bar** - a known
violation that ships today, independent of int8. That is decision 1, and it is not a speed/quality
trade.

## Frame 475 was a legitimate reuse

The render's own log line: `reuse frame=475 depth=1 share=0.06887` - below the 0.07 threshold. The
0.07108 quoted earlier was the **simulation's** value, not the gate's; the contradiction was in my
labelling, not in the product.

## The peak is the material's

In the space the bar is defined in (the decoded sweep), Violet's int8 peak is **146** (frame 330), and
fp16 under the same gate peaks at **149** - higher. 192 frames exceed 20 levels and 24 exceed 55. So
the "peak 128" was an RGB-space number, and the peak is a property of the material rather than an int8
increment.
