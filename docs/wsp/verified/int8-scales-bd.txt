# Can int8's accuracy be fixed? Yes - one lever, and it is the boundary scale, not the clip

Measured 2026-11-18 on the BD Violet clip (`bd_violet20s.mkv`, 37.715 Mbps, packet-proved `-c copy`).
Frames: 1080p RGB dumps at 20, 29, 44, 60, 100, 140, 180, 220, 260, 274, 300. Calibration on the eight
frames 20/60/100/140/180/220/260/300, test on 29, 44 and 274 - **disjoint sets**, which is what makes the
result a prediction rather than a fit.

Instrument: `scripts/int8_accuracy_sim.py` (the sim that produced every recorded int8 accuracy number),
extended with `--act-scale {tensor,channel}` and `--clip PCT`; defaults unchanged, so the recorded recipe
is what runs when nobody asks.

## The anchor holds first

| policy (frame 29) | share > 8 | peak | median |
|---|---|---|---|
| **per-tensor activations (the recorded recipe)** | **0.06510%** | 19 | 2.99 |

That reproduces the recorded simulation exactly, so the extensions below are read against the same
instrument rather than against a new one.

## The two levers, one at a time

| frame | per-tensor (baseline) | **per-channel activations** | per-tensor + p99.9 clip |
|---|---|---|---|
| 29 (worst in the app) | 0.06510% | **0.00162%** | 0.37460% |
| 44 | 0.02461% | **0.00187%** | 0.38626% |
| 274 | 0.01362% | **0.00118%** | 0.30799% |

- **Per-channel activation scales are worth 15-40x** on every frame, and they also collapse the peak
  (19 -> 13) and the median (2.99 -> 0.75 levels). This is the same lever the BD diff pointed at: the
  error sits at the ends of the range, and one scale for all 64 channels spends its codes on the union.
- **Percentile clipping makes it 6-16x WORSE.** The tail is not clipping noise, it is signal: the
  activations genuinely use the range, so giving up the last 0.1% costs far more than it buys. That lever
  is closed by measurement.

## What this does and does not establish

The sim says the scale granularity is the knob, and that on this material it moves the worst frame by
more than an order of magnitude. It does **not** yet say int8 passes on the product: the kernel reads
**0.53318%** on the same frame (frame 29 of the 494-frame render), i.e. **8x above this sim's own
baseline**. That gap is unexplained and it is the same size and direction as the granularity effect, so
the first hypothesis is that the kernel's hop boundary requantises with a per-tensor scale while the sim
models that boundary per-channel. Until the kernel is measured with per-channel boundary scales, the
number to quote for the product is 0.53%, not 0.0016%.

The kernel change is small in principle: the epilogue already applies a per-channel output scale, and a
per-channel requant to int8 for the next hop is one multiply plus 64 scales (128 bytes) per hop.

## Not covered

- Three test frames of one title. The app's worst frame is one of them, which is why they were chosen,
  but a 494-frame sweep of the changed kernel is the acceptance, not this table.
- The edge class (8-bit rounding on 2-3 px lines) is not addressed by any scale policy - the diff shows
  it as 55-79% of the defect, and only precision moves it.
- The sim rounds in float; the kernel accumulates in int32 (which is exact for int8 operands), so that
  difference is not a candidate for the 8x gap.
