# Can int8's accuracy be fixed? Yes - one lever, and it is the boundary scale, not the clip

Measured 2026-11-18 on the BD Violet clip (`bd_violet20s.mkv`, 37.715 Mbps, packet-proved `-c copy`).
Frames: 1080p RGB dumps at 20, 29, 44, 60, 100, 140, 180, 220, 260, 274, 300. Calibration on the eight
frames 20/60/100/140/180/220/260/300, test on 29, 44 and 274 - **disjoint sets**, which is what makes the
result a prediction rather than a fit.

Instrument: `scripts/int8_accuracy_sim.py` (the sim that produced every recorded int8 accuracy number),
extended with `--act-scale {tensor,channel}` and `--clip PCT`; defaults unchanged, so the recorded recipe
is what runs when nobody asks.

## The anchor holds first

| policy (frame 29) | share > 8 | peak | median |
|---|---|---|---|
| **per-tensor activations (the recorded recipe)** | **0.06510%** | 19 | 2.99 |

That reproduces the recorded simulation exactly, so the extensions below are read against the same
instrument rather than against a new one.

## The two levers, one at a time

| frame | per-tensor (baseline) | **per-channel activations** | per-tensor + p99.9 clip |
|---|---|---|---|
| 29 (worst in the app) | 0.06510% | **0.00162%** | 0.37460% |
| 44 | 0.02461% | **0.00187%** | 0.38626% |
| 274 | 0.01362% | **0.00118%** | 0.30799% |

- **Per-channel activation scales are worth 15-40x** on every frame, and they also collapse the peak
  (19 -> 13) and the median (2.99 -> 0.75 levels). This is the same lever the BD diff pointed at: the
  error sits at the ends of the range, and one scale for all 64 channels spends its codes on the union.
- **Percentile clipping makes it 6-16x WORSE.** The tail is not clipping noise, it is signal: the
  activations genuinely use the range, so giving up the last 0.1% costs far more than it buys. That lever
  is closed by measurement.

## What this does and does not establish

The sim says the scale granularity is the knob, and that on this material it moves the worst frame by
more than an order of magnitude. It does **not** yet say int8 passes on the product: the kernel reads
**0.53318%** on the same frame (frame 29 of the 494-frame render), i.e. **8x above this sim's own
baseline**. That gap is unexplained and it is the same size and direction as the granularity effect, so
the first hypothesis is that the kernel's hop boundary requantises with a per-tensor scale while the sim
models that boundary per-channel. Until the kernel is measured with per-channel boundary scales, the
number to quote for the product is 0.53%, not 0.0016%.

The kernel change is small in principle: the epilogue already applies a per-channel output scale, and a
per-channel requant to int8 for the next hop is one multiply plus 64 scales (128 bytes) per hop.

## Not covered

- Three test frames of one title. The app's worst frame is one of them, which is why they were chosen,
  but a 494-frame sweep of the changed kernel is the acceptance, not this table.
- The edge class (8-bit rounding on 2-3 px lines) is not addressed by any scale policy - the diff shows
  it as 55-79% of the defect, and only precision moves it.
- The sim rounds in float; the kernel accumulates in int32 (which is exact for int8 operands), so that
  difference is not a candidate for the 8x gap.

## The kernel, measured: the lever does not transfer

The simulation's per-channel lever was implemented in the kernel the way the arithmetic allows - the next
hop's grid is no longer collapsed to `max(s_in)` and repacked with an m multiplier; the per-input-channel
scale is folded into the weights at build time, which makes the epilogue requant the identity and removes
a multiply from the hot path (`backend/src/pytorch/Int8Body.py`, opt-in `RVE_INT8_PER_CHANNEL=1`).

Same clip, same pipe on both sides, same fp16 reference, 494 frames:

| variant | worst share | frames over the bar | worst block mean | peak |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| per-tensor (shipped) | **0.41998%** (frame 274) | 356 / 494 | 5.91 | 59 |
| per-channel (new) | 1.03183% against the older rgb24 reference; see below | 472 / 494 | 7.92 | 74 |
| **per-channel against per-tensor** | **0.01541%** (frame 42) | **0** | 2.51 | 19 |

The flag was verified to be live, not merely parsed: inspecting the built launches, `m` (the epilogue's
requant multiplier) is `1 << 20` - the identity - on every hop under `RVE_INT8_PER_CHANNEL=1`, against
47396..1048576 under the default, and the per-output-channel weight scales move from 0.00179..0.01475 to
0.00062..0.00686, i.e. the input scale really is inside the weights.

The last row is the one that answers the question: the two int8 variants differ by **0.015% of pixels**,
with no frame over the bar between them. The boundary scale is therefore not the kernel's error source -
whatever costs the ~0.4-0.9% is shared by both variants and is not the granularity the simulation
isolated.

That leaves the instrument: the sim reads 0.06510% on frame 29 and the kernel reads 0.42-0.91% on the same
clip, with the product's own probe agreeing with the kernel (engine 0.688-0.824%). The sim is the odd one
out by 10-14x, and until that is explained no sim-proposed lever should be patched into the kernel - the
one that was cost 0.015% of picture, not the 40x the sim promised. That is queued as
`rve-int8-sim-vs-kernel-2026-11-18`.

Frames for the eye, `/home/jorj/eye-check/2026-11-19-int8-per-channel/` (frame 274, 1:1 crops, diffs x16):
`src_f274_crop.png` is the SOURCE read at 1080p and scaled x2 nearest, so the original ink can be compared
with what the model drew; `src_fp16_int8_f274_montage.png` puts source | fp16 | int8 side by side;
`pt_vs_fp16_f274_montage.png` and `pc_vs_pt_f42_montage.png` are the two diffs. On all of them the int8
error is the line art, not the flat fills.

Caveat on the absolute numbers: the 0.41998% row was measured with the yuv420p pipe on both sides (the
default since this morning), while the earlier 0.53318% was an rgb24-pipe comparison on a 480-frame
population. Both are int8 against fp16 on the same title; they are not the same population, and this file
does not divide one by the other.

## Through the shipping encode, the difference is smaller than the codec's own error

The bar is measured on the internal render (ffv1). The product ships an encode. So the population that
decides whether a viewer sees anything is the DECODED file, and that was measured for both settings the
owner asked about - `hevc_nvenc -profile:v main10 -cq:v 18 -tune uhq`, 3840x2160, no scaling, i.e. the
product's own `-cq:v` knob with 10-bit output:

| frame 169 | mean \|d\| | peak | share > 8 |
|---|---|---|---|
| int8 encode vs its own render | **2.107** | 14 | 0.0405% |
| fp16 encode vs its own render | **2.071** | 14 | 0.0316% |
| the A/B on the renders | **0.777** | - | 0.2059% |
| the A/B on the decoded encodes | **0.642** | - | 0.2075% |

Two readings, both needed:

- the **codec's own error is 2.7x the difference being judged** (2.1 against 0.78 levels mean), and the
  correlation between the render-level difference field and the decoded-level one is **0.322**: the
  encoder moves the ink about as much as int8 does. A viewer cannot attribute what they see on the file to
  the quantiser;
- the **count above the bar survives** (0.2059% -> 0.2075%), because the int8 difference is concentrated on
  edges while the codec's error is diffuse. So the number does not vanish; it stops being attributable.

Consequence for the verdict: on this material int8 and fp16 are not distinguishable in the delivered video,
which matches the owner's eye ("no difference at all"). The 0.1% bar remains a valid instrument for the
internal render, but it is not a shipping criterion for this class - the encode spends more than the
quantiser does. A lossless 4K comparison (yuv444p) is exact (mean 0.000, correlation 1.000) but unplayable
in real time on the card that renders it, so it is a record, not a viewing instrument.

## What int8 actually costs the drawing: the stroke loses contrast, the colour does not

The owner's report was "fp16 reproduces the halo, int8 does not, and the tints are unclear". Both halves are
measurable, and they came out as: colour is fine, the STROKE is what changes.

Method (`scripts/tint_attribution.py`): take the model's own fp16 output, find the pixels on the top 2% of
its laplacian - the line art - and split them into the ink core (laplacian < 0, the dark line) and the outer
rim (laplacian > 0, the lighter side next to it). Then read each render's mean luma on those two sets,
because a halo is a signed effect and an absolute difference cannot see it.

| frame | image | ink core | outer rim | ink - model | rim - model |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 169 | model | 77.60 | 143.20 | - | - |
| 169 | fp16 engine | 78.68 | 144.64 | **+1.07** | **+1.43** |
| 169 | int8 body | 82.00 | 143.57 | **+4.40** | +0.36 |
| 274 | model | 58.63 | 149.82 | - | - |
| 274 | fp16 engine | 59.65 | 151.36 | **+1.02** | **+1.54** |
| 274 | int8 body | 65.20 | 149.38 | **+6.57** | -0.44 |

Read it as: the fp16 engine lifts the line AND the rim by about the same amount, so the model's halo survives
intact (the ink-to-rim step is 65.6 in the model, 65.9 in the engine). The int8 body lifts the ink by 4.4-6.6
levels and leaves the rim where it was (or lowers it), which cuts the ink-to-rim step by ~7-9% - the line
goes pale, the halo flattens, and the drawing reads softer. That is the class the owner sees, and it is not
the class the 0.1% share describes: the share counts pixels 8 levels off, this is a systematic +5 on the ink.

Colour is the opposite story: chroma against the source is 0.30-0.45 levels mean for both, and against the
model each render's block-mean RGB shift is within 0.06 levels of the other's; on 82-87% of blocks int8 is
marginally CLOSER to the source colour than fp16 (five frames). So "tints" are not where int8 differs - the
stroke is.

Consequence: 8-bit cannot fix this class either - it is a systematic lift of the ink, produced by quantising
a 2-3 px excursion, and per-channel scales moved it by 0.015% (the section above). If the pale stroke is
acceptable the lane ships on picture; if it is not, the fix is precision at the edges, not a scale.

## The scale axis is exhausted, and the ledger says what is left

A second pass over `harness.cli db lane int8` (2026-11-18) - the owner asked whether everything had been
tried - separates four axes that were being mixed:

**Closed by measurement, before today:** TRT's int8 two-pass (0.82x, its epilogue cannot take a per-channel
slope); asymmetric/uint8 with the zero-point in the bias (0.102% at peak 32 against 0.058% at peak 37 - worse
on share, better on peak); per-output-channel weights (shipped); per-tap weight scales (finer, measured 2.47x
in TIME and it breaks the chain because one int32 accumulator spans all nine taps); rounding mode
(round-half-even matched to torch, worth 1.23% of pixels when it was wrong); per-title calibration split (a
correctness condition, 20x mis-statement if shared).

**Closed today:** per-channel activation scales (+0.015% of picture), percentile clipping (6-16x worse),
grid margin (x1.25 doubles the error, and x1.5 makes the body's own self-certification refuse and fall back
to fp16 - the safeguard firing, not a win).

**Not closed, and the only axes left:**
1. **precision allocation inside the chain** - keep the LAST k conv pairs in fp16, because a late hop's error
   is not diluted by anything downstream. Simulated on frame 169: 0.00633% (k=0) -> 0.00508 (k=2) -> 0.00313
   (k=4) -> 0.00209 (k=6), median 1.59 -> 1.00 levels. A ~3x on the sim's already-small number, at the cost
   of k slower fp16 hops (1.8-3 ms each) - it trades the speed that is the whole reason for int8.
2. **the sim/kernel gap (6-14x)** - unfixed, and it makes every sim-proposed lever unverifiable in the
   kernel, which is exactly what the last three attempts demonstrated. This is the row to open before any
   more accuracy work.

**Found on the way, and worth recording as behaviour:** the body self-certifies on the first frame and
refuses itself above 0.1% ("int8 body self-certification ... 0.28825% -> refusing, fp16 engine runs"). That is
a second, independent guard besides the probe, and it is what made the margin-x1.5 run render fp16 with a
0.00000% difference - not a quality result.

## The correction that matters: against the MODEL the body is fine, and the pipe is the floor

The emulation of the kernel's own quantiser in torch (`scripts/int8_kernel_emulation.py`) reproduces the body
**bit-exactly** (share 0.00000%, peak 0.0 on every frame tested), which is what makes the comparison below
trustworthy rather than another cross-instrument guess. With the reference set to the model's own fp16 output
(torch, RGB, no colour pipe on either side):

| frame | 29 | 44 | 100 | 169 | 274 | 400 | 460 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **int8 body vs model** | 0.01684% | 0.01519% | 0.00505% | 0.00914% | 0.01917% | 0.00762% | 0.00442% |
| fp16 engine vs model (raw) | 0.49237% | 0.49345% | 0.26442% | 0.26794% | 0.46212% | 0.23527% | 0.10531% |

The engine column is not the engine's fault: pushing BOTH sides through the same 4:2:0 round trip drops it
to 0.087% (frame 29) and 0.055% (169), and **the pipe alone** - the model against itself through the same
round trip - reads **0.21053%** and **0.12868%** (`scripts/engine_vs_model_control.py`).

Three consequences, in order of how much they change the record:

1. **int8 passes against the model with 5-25x margin.** Every "int8 fails" number in this file (0.42-0.53%)
   was body-against-ENGINE on delivered files, so it measured the engine's deviation plus the colour pipe.
   The verdict is superseded by `int8-body-vs-model`.
2. **The acceptance statistic cannot resolve anything below ~0.2% on a delivered 4:2:0 file.** The colour
   pipe moves that many pixels past the bar by itself on this material. It stays a valid render-internal
   instrument and an invalid delivery one - which is also what the encode comparison found independently.
3. **The engine is the less faithful renderer here**: 0.055-0.087% from the weights against the body's
   0.004-0.019%, i.e. 3-10x. Whether that is tactics, fp16 accumulation or the padded head is not measured;
   recorded as `engine-vs-model-uncertain`.

What still costs the body its character is the stroke: the ink lifts +4.4/+6.6 levels while the rim stays,
a signed effect that sits below the 8-level bar and is therefore invisible to every number above.
