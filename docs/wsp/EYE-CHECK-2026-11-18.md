Two orders of magnitude between two clips, so int8 is not "broken" or "fine" - its accuracy is set by the
per-clip activation grids. For comparison, the recorded int8 number on the 4 Mbps re-encode of Violet was
0.08876% with zero frames above the bar: that pass belonged to the smooth material.

The probe agrees in direction on Violet (composition d1 0.266-0.918%, peak 25-68) and disagrees on the
Chainsaw cuts (0.004-0.015%, peak 25) - **but the probe's pair population changes with the gate
configuration** (7 pairs on Violet, 4 on the battle cut, 2 on the same-shot cut, and the same clip reads
engine 0.082% in an fp16 run against 0.688% in an int8-forced run), so probe numbers are only comparable
inside one configuration. That is a defect of the probe worth its own row, not something to average.

**Question for the eye:** once the blind A/B is rendered, can you pick the int8 frame at all on the
Chainsaw shot? If not, the probe's 0.004-0.015% there is the number that matters, and the Violet refusal
is the product's correct behaviour.
