# The curvature-priced predictor picks K=4 for every model, and agrees with the ladder once in three

Measured 2026-11-20, three models, three held-out frames, crops calibration. Records
`int8-price-predictor.jsonl` (PR001-PR010), raw `logs/price-*.json`, probe `harness/scripts/price_predictor_probe.py`.

`Predicting Quantization Price` (arXiv:2609.28270) says the correct pre-deployment score is the candidate's
layer-output error covariance weighted by the reference model's **downstream curvature**, and that reconstruction
and diagonal scores fail to rank configurations because they drop that curvature. Our linear sum of the two measured
errors ranked the ladder 4/22. This is the class the paper says is right, built and scored:

```
price(K) = sum_l mse_l(K) * c_l
mse_l  error of layer l's quantised output, from the body's own grids at that K
c_l    mean squared change of the FINAL output per unit perturbation of layer l (finite difference, 5% of the
       layer's spread, one random direction)
```

## Result

| model | price K=2 | price K=3 | price K=4 | predicted | ladder's best |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| realesr-animevideov3 | 1.015e-5 | 5.69e-6 | **4.47e-6** | K=4 | K=3 (0.00709%) |
| 2x_Ani4K_Compact | 9.28e-6 | 5.42e-6 | **4.24e-6** | K=4 | K=4 (0.00535%) |
| 2x-Futsuu-Anime | 9.16e-6 | 5.07e-6 | **3.88e-6** | K=4 | K=2 (0.00047%) |

**The score is monotone in K on all three models** - it always prefers the finest grid - and it agrees with the
ladder once in three, by luck on the one model whose ladder optimum happens to be K=4. On Futsuu it picks K=4 where
the ladder says K=2 (0.00353% against 0.00047%, seven times worse); on realesr it picks K=4 where the ladder says
K=3 (0.04456% against 0.00709%, six times worse).

## Why the "correct" price still fails here

The price is derived from a forward-KL (an L2-type) drift, and our acceptance statistic is not that: it is the
**share of pixels whose channel-max error exceeds 8 levels**. A finer grid lowers the L2 error of every layer while
scattering more small errors across pixels; whether that raises or lowers the count of pixels above a threshold is
a different question, and on these models the count gets worse as the L2 gets better. So the priced score is
correct for its own target and still ranks our target wrongly - which is the strongest form of the negative result
this lane can record: not "we could not predict K", but "the class the literature says is correct does not predict
K either, because the objective it prices is not the one we decide on".

## Verdict

Row `rve-price-curvature-predictor-2026-11-20` closes as **refuted**: 1/3 agreement, and the one agreement is a
constant predictor landing on a model whose optimum is that constant. The sweep stays - with its K=2-clean skip,
which is what keeps its cost at ~0 on the models that do not need it.

## What is NOT claimed

- **Three models, one direction per layer.** The curvature estimate is a one-direction finite difference; a
  multi-direction estimate would sharpen `c_l` but cannot change the sign of a score that is monotone in K by
  construction (finer grid, lower mse, and `c_l` is K-independent).
- **The paper's full machinery was not reproduced** - its budgeted search over configuration families is not
  needed to score three K values, and the trace reduction it describes is what this implements.
