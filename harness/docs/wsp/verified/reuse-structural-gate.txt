# Reuse decided on the drawing: a coarse statistic, a warped emission, and 63.8% of a calm window reused

Measured 2026-11-20/21 (local), the app's own renderer (TensorRT, 2x Futsuu Anime, 1440x1080 -> 2880x2160, ffv1
lossless sink). Queue row: `rve-reuse-structural-gate-2026-11-20`. Evidence trail that led here:
`harness/docs/wsp/FINDINGS-reuse-boiling-material-2026-11-20.md`.

The row's premise, from that trail: the shipped statistic (max over 32 px blocks of the mean |difference| at full
resolution) qualifies 6.7% of consecutive pairs on calm Pink Panther material, and a 2 px low-pass plus a
translation estimate qualifies 47.8% - because a 0.5 px shift of a 1-2 px black line moves edge pixels by tens of
levels. So the gate was measuring the registration of the scan, not whether the cel changed.

## What was built

**Decision** (`summary statistic = "coarse"`, `backend/src/utils/TemporalReuse.py`): luma at half resolution, a
3 px Gaussian low-pass (in LR pixels), FFT phase correlation against the reference (Hann-windowed, sub-pixel by a
parabolic fit, refused below a response floor or above a shift limit), one bilinear warp of the reference, then the
same max-over-32-px-blocks block-mean statistic. The decision takes the **minimum of the warped and the un-warped
comparison**, so the registration estimate can only lower the statistic - a biased or refused estimate degrades to
the shipped geometry instead of inventing damage.

**Emission** (`--temporal_reuse_warp`, `TemporalCorrection.align`): the cached HR picture and its LR frame are moved
by the gate's own estimate before the residual correction, so the residual is not asked to undo a sub-pixel wobble
and the drawing does not stay where the previous frame put it. The shift applied is the *increment* against the
previous frame, not the shift from the reference (a chain serves a frame from up to `max_chain` frames back).

**Certification** (`RenderVideo._calibrate_gate`): the acceptance statistic is taken at the SAME coarse scale as the
decision, and it prices the **emission** (base moved by the accumulated shift, plus the LR residual the corrector
adds) rather than the stale frame. The pixel-scale share is computed and logged beside it, as dust.

## Measurements

### The statistic, on the gate's own population (LR input, native 24 fps)

`harness/scripts/reuse_structural_probe.py` runs BOTH state machines over the same decoded frames - reference
keeping, chain cap 4, scene cut - because a share of PAIRS is not a share of frames.

| window (denoised `Untitled78.mov`) | shipped, threshold 4 / 5 | coarse, threshold 2 / 3 / 4 |
|---|---|---|
| calm, t=120, 72 frames | 12.5% / 23.6% | **30.6% / 58.3% / 65.3%** |
| fast motion, t=280, 72 frames | 4.2% / 4.2% | 5.6% / 20.8% / 31.9% |

The statistic separates the two content classes (5.5x at threshold 2) where the shipped one does not (both 4.2%).
Registration was refused on 0 of 71 comparisons on the calm window.

So at the scale the decision is taken at, the reused frames are inside the acceptance bar with a 3x margin, and the
pixel-scale number that fails the old bar is 90% dust-dot area plus the scan's registration - the two things the
owner said need not be preserved ("после денойза зерна нет, есть точки то есть то нет, и кадр подрагивает из-за
скана"). That decision is what moves the certifier to the coarse scale; the pixel number is reported, never used
as a bar for this statistic.

### The geometry alone, as the row asked to see it: the apparent shift of a gated render

Item (b) of the row: "measuring the apparent shift of a gated render against an ungated one: it must read ~0 at the
coarse scale". `reuse_structural_probe.py --shift-pair` registers every frame of the pair on the coarse luma:

| render | median (\|dx\|, \|dy\|) | max | frames over 0.3 px |
|---|---|---|---|
| coarse statistic, warp ON | **(0.001, 0.001) px** | 0.128 px | **0 of 80** |
| coarse statistic, `--no_temporal_reuse_warp` | (0.007, 0.001) px | **0.476 px** | **7 of 80** |

So without the warp the drawing sits up to half an upscaled pixel away on a reused frame - small on a still, but it
is a jump between a reused and a fresh frame, which is what the row refused to accept. Registration is a
where-the-picture-is number and says nothing about photometric damage; the pair table above covers that.

### Cost (`harness/scripts/reuse_coarse_cost.py`, 1440x1080, medians of 30 rounds after 5 warm-ups)

| | |
|---|---|
| `is_duplicate`, shipped `mean_abs` | 1.06 ms |
| `is_duplicate`, `coarse` | **1.49 ms** (+0.43) |
| emission warp at 2880x2160x3 | **0.81 ms** |
| per reused frame | **2.30 ms** against ~39 ms of model call |

The row's target was <= 2 ms; the measured 2.30 is above it and is reported as such. The falsifier ("registration +
warp costs more than the frames it saves") is not close: at 63.8% reuse the saving is ~25 ms/frame average.

### Controls

* **Identity**: a frame against itself reads exactly 0.000 in both statistics and registers a shift of 0.
* **A/A**: two ungated renders of the same cut (`off1`, `off2`) decode to identical frames (80/80). The container
  bytes differ (timestamps), the pictures do not - so a `config` comparison is measuring the gate, not the encoder.
* **A synthetic sub-pixel shift on real line art**: 0.35 px reads 7.34-8.82 in the shipped statistic and 1.2-1.8 in
  the coarse one, and a +12-level area change still reads 12.0 (a statistic that accepts everything is not a
  statistic).
* **The emission decomposition** (5 consecutive pairs of a real render pair, coarse / pixel): stale frame
  0.343% / 4.70%, moved 0.181% / 3.86%, **moved + residual 0.000% / 2.85%**. That is why the certifier had to price
  the emission: on the stale frame it read 0.40% and refused material the emission handles.

## The three bugs this cost, all found by measurement rather than by reading

1. `grid_sample` reads the INPUT at the grid's coordinates, so an offset of +d moves the content by **-d**. Both
   warps had the sign backwards; a dot moved by +3 landed at -3, and a synthetic shift *increased* the statistic.
2. The sub-pixel parabola was written `(right - left)` on the x axis and `(down - up)` on the y one - one of the two
   is the wrong direction, and a +0.4 px move read back as -0.2 px. Pinned now by `test_coarse_registration_...`.
3. The emission warp subtracted the shift twice (once in `arange`, once in the grid), moving the picture by twice
   the estimate. Caught by a dot test after the render pair had already been measured - the reported numbers are
   from the fixed build.

## Falsifier

Not triggered. The coarse statistic lifts the reusable share on calm windows to 63.8% (target >= 30%) with the
coarse-scale damage at 0.030% (bar 0.1%), and registration + warp cost 2.30 ms against a ~39 ms model call.

## What this does NOT cover

* **One clip, one model, one scale.** 078 Pink Plasma, calm window only (t=120-123.4 s), 2x Futsuu Anime at 1080p.
  The fast-motion refusal is measured on the LR simulation (5.6% at threshold 2), not on a render.
* **Dust is counted by area**, not by visibility: a dot's area and count, not whether a viewer notices it. The
  owner's decision that dust need not be preserved is what makes the coarse bar legitimate here; it is not a
  measurement of how the result looks.
* **The pixel-scale bar still applies to every other statistic.** `mean_abs` and `channel_max_share` are untouched
  and still certify at the pixel scale.
* **The chain's geometry is accumulated from per-frame estimates**, whose bias is ~40% low on broad peaks; the
  warp is what the statistic certified, not an exact registration.
* **Rotation and scale of the scan are not modelled** - translation only.
* **Memory bound**: the acceptance certifier keeps the last `depth+1` LR frames within a 64 MB budget, so on 4K
  input it prices only depth 1 and logs that; deeper chains are not certified there.
* **4x models** pay ~5.9 ms for the emission warp (measured), which is not covered by the 2.30 ms figure.
