# The hop is at its floor on this shape: neither the load path nor the weight traffic moves it

Measured 2026-11-20 (local), RTX 4060 Ti, one hop of the int8 body on its own codes (frame 169 of BD Violet),
three arms in one process, 3 interleaved rounds, medians of rounds 2+.
Records: `int8-hop-loads.jsonl`; raw: `logs/hop-schedule/probe.json`.

Row `rve-hop-load-schedule-2026-11-19` (researcher's C3). The diagnosis it rests on: the hop runs 1.68 ms,
which is 152.9 GFLOP / 1.68 = **91 TOPS** and 265 MB / 1.68 = **158 GB/s** - ~52-55% of *both* of this
card's roofs at once. A kernel at half of two roofs is bound by neither, so the suspicion was the schedule:
nine masked gathers per block with no 128-bit vectorisation, and weights re-read by every CTA (~0.58 GB of
L2 traffic per hop, derived).

## Three arms

| arm | ms | TOPS | % of the 176 | GB/s | % of the 288 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| today, BLOCK_M 128 | **1.756** | 87.1 | 49.5% | 150.9 | 52.4% |
| + `max_contiguous`/`multiple_of` on the lanes | 1.772 | 86.3 | 49.0% | 149.6 | 51.9% |
| two row-tiles per CTA, weights loaded once for both | 2.696 | 56.7 | 32.2% | 98.3 | 34.1% |

**Nothing moves it up.** The vectorisation hint is +0.9%, i.e. nothing: the compiler was already producing
the same accesses, which is what the shipped comment ("the gathers are already L2-resident") claimed and had
never been tested against a hint. The weight hoist is **54% slower**: two 128x64 int32 accumulators per CTA
spill, and the spill costs more than the halved weight traffic saves - so the weight traffic was not the
binding term either. All three arms produce **0 differing codes** of 132.7 M, so the comparison is one
schedule against another and not one arithmetic against another.

## What this closes

The row's own falsifier: *"If neither a vectorised/block-pointer load nor a weight-stationary CTA moves the
achieved TOPS off 91, the shape is at its floor and the largest line in the frame is closed."* Both were
tried and neither moved it, so **no known lever remains unmeasured on the tap side** - see `int8-hop-roof.txt`
for why that is not a floor: a 0.59 ms per-launch constant sits outside the taps entirely. The account of
why the two roofs are both at ~50% is now open in a different way than the row assumed: not "the schedule is
wasteful" but "the shape costs one of each per element and the two costs do not overlap" - the 9 taps read
per pixel and the single 64-byte write are both irreducible at int8.

Together with `int8-sub8-codes` (6-bit codes: 2.7% of pixels, dead on the picture) the hop line has now been
attacked from both ends - schedule and traffic - and both are closed. What is left in the frame is not in the
hop.

## Not covered

- One card, one hop, one frame's codes. BLOCK_M was not re-swept here: the record has 128 -> 256 as 1.82
  against 2.20 ms and 512 spilling (79 ms), so the sweep is not the open question.
- `tl.make_block_ptr` was not used: a 3x3 shifted window is not a block, and constructing three row blocks
  per tap is the same nine loads with extra index arithmetic. The `max_contiguous` hint is the load-path
  change the compiler can act on, and it was measured.
- One persistent-CTA variant (a grid smaller than the SM count, each CTA looping over tiles) is NOT tried -
  that is the one remaining schedule idea that does not add accumulators, and it is named here rather than
  implied.
- Nothing here claims another card behaves the same.
