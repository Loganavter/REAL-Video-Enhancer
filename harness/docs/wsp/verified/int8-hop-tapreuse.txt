# Tap reuse in Triton: the idea is expressible, and `tl.gather` costs thirteen times what it saves

Measured 2026-11-19 (local), one hop of the int8 body on its own codes (frame 169), three interleaved rounds,
medians of rounds 2+. Records: `int8-hop-tapreuse.jsonl`; raw: `logs/hop-tapreuse/probe.json`; kernel:
`harness/scripts/hop_tapreuse_probe.py`.

The premise was the strongest one left in the queue (`rve-hop-fuse2-cascade`, re-filed as tap reuse): the hop
reads every pixel **nine times** - 9 x 132.71 MB = 1194 MB of logical reads - and L2 sees 1528.7 MB, i.e. 128% of
that, so coalescing and the NHWC layout are fine; **LSU is the highest-utilised pipe at 73.9%**. Six families of
schedule knobs (BLOCK_M, warps, cache policy, tile shape, channel split, batching) were tried around those nine
gathers and all lost; none touched the reads. If the reads fell, the hop could approach its compute roof, and
sixteen hops would go from 24.30 to 13.90 ms.

The blocker used to be that Triton has no explicit shared memory. **Triton 3.5 has `tl.gather`**, which makes the
horizontal shift expressible inside a tile, so the family was reopened: load the three rows once, form the nine
taps by shifting in registers.

## Measured

| arm | ms | TOPS | vs today |
|---|---|---|---|
| today, nine gathers | **1.478** | 103.4 | - |
| three rows loaded once + nine register gathers, BLOCK_M 128 | **19.895** | 7.7 | **-1246%** |
| the same at BLOCK_M 64 | 11.928 | 12.8 | -707% |

Codes identical in both arms (0 of 132.7 M differ), so this is the same arithmetic through a different data path.

**`tl.gather` costs thirteen times the whole kernel.** It lowers to a lane permutation - in effect a shared-memory
round trip per element - and doing nine of them per output row is far more expensive than nine L1/L2-resident
loads, which is exactly what the current kernel does. The mechanism (fewer reads) is real; the only in-language
way to build it is not.

## What the power-of-two restriction cost on top

`tl.arange` requires a power of two, so the three-row band cannot be BLOCK_M+2 wide: it was loaded 2*BLOCK_M wide
with two lanes wasted, which caps this formulation at **1.5x fewer reads** (6 per pixel against 9) rather than the
3.05x a hand-written band gets, or the 8x that vertical reuse across tiles would. So even had the gather been
free, Triton's arithmetic would have delivered half the available reduction.

## Where this leaves the family

| route | status |
|---|---|
| Triton, register gathers | **closed here**: `tl.gather` 13x the kernel, and the band is capped at 1.5x by `tl.arange` |
| Triton, pipelined taps (`num_stages`) | closed earlier: 2.497 / 2.635 ms against 2.198 |
| Triton, reordered loads / hoisted weights | closed earlier: -101% and -54% |
| CUDA with explicit shared memory | **not measured on the real shape**; a microbenchmark of the same mechanism was -26% |

So the one big lever that would attack the actual limiter now rests entirely on a hand-written kernel, and every
in-language expression of it has been measured and lost. That is a stronger statement than "closed" - it is the
boundary of the instrument, and it took one session to establish rather than the two the row budgeted.

## Not covered

- One card, one hop, one frame's codes; `tl.gather`'s cost is a Triton 3.5 codegen fact, not a hardware one.
- The CUDA kernel is not written. Its prior is unfavourable (the microbenchmark) but its ceiling is the largest
  in the queue (-10.4 ms), so it remains a decision rather than a closed question.

## The mechanism, measured: staging moves the wrong counter

An outside reader supplied the explanation this family needed, and the counters confirm it. The hop's instruction
mix, measured with ncu on the shipped kernel (one launch, frame 169):

```
sm__inst_executed.sum             230 752 800 inst
sm__inst_executed_pipe_lsu.sum     52 358 400 inst   = 22.7% of all instructions
gpu__time_duration.sum                    1.52 ms
```

**Shared-memory staging does not reduce the instructions that hit the LSU - it increases them.** A staged variant
pays a global load, an smem store and an smem load where today pays one global load (which is L1/L2-resident
anyway, at a 91% L2 hit rate):

| variant | global loads | smem stores | smem loads | LSU instructions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| today | 9 per output pixel | 0 | 0 | **9** |
| 2-D tile T=32 (1.13x reads) | 1.13 | 1.13 | 9 | 11.26 (**+25%**) |
| 3-row band, BM 128 (3.05x reads) | 3.05 | 3.05 | 9 | 15.09 (**+68%**) |

LSU is already the busiest pipe (73.9% of its issue slots). Staging buys a reduction in L2 *traffic* - which is
healthy at 128% of the logical volume - and pays for it in the one resource that is saturating. That single
sentence predicts, without any theory, the three measurements already on this page: the register-gather route
13.5x slower, the pipelined taps -12/-20%, the CUDA microbenchmark of cooperative staging -26%.

The register route fails for a different and equally structural reason: in an mma layout, adjacent spatial
positions live in different lanes, so intra-register reuse is *inter-lane*, and `tl.gather` prices that at 13.5x.
A register-local alternative (per-thread spatial blocks, dp4a) has a roof of 34 SM x 64 INT32 x 8 ops x 2.54 GHz =
**44.2 TOPS**, against the 103.4 the mma kernel already reaches - i.e. the non-tensor-core escape is 2.3x worse
than what we have.

So the family is closed **by mechanism**: the only levers that reduce reads pay in the saturated resource, and the
only lever that avoids that costs more arithmetic than it saves. What the -10.4 ms ceiling actually required was
removing the FIRST tap's exposed latency (0.590 ms, 37% of the hop) - a latency-hiding problem, which staging does
not touch, because the first data still has to arrive.
